\section{Relation to Prior Work}\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Free completion and partial algebras}
The free compatible completion underlying \cref{sec:free-completion} belongs to
an established line of work on partial algebras and Schmidt-type completions
\cite{ClimentVidalCosmeLlopez2023}.  Diaconescu's many-sorted encoding provides
a closely related partial-to-total perspective \cite{Diaconescu2009}.  The substrate used here is that theory, specialized to the one-sorted binary
case; \cref{sec:diaconescu-bridge} makes the specialization explicit.

\subsection{Recognizable trees, discrimination, and finite separators}
For finite bases and finite signatures, the normalized ground-equational
presentation lies within classical tree-automata and recognizability theory
\cite{Kozen1977,Brainerd1969,DauchetTison1990,ComonEtAl2008}.  Finite selected
subterm closures plus a sink state are familiar constructions.  Likewise, the
abstract requirement that one homomorphism be injective on a prescribed finite
set belongs to the established language of discrimination and fully residual
algebras.  The relative feature studied here is therefore more restrictive:
every observer must preserve an arbitrary partial base pointwise while only the
complement over that base is budgeted.  This permits infinite codomains with
finite external complexity.

This restriction matters structurally.  The usual residual argument can combine
pairwise separators by taking their Cartesian product.  That operation does not
preserve the present observer class over an infinite fixed base.  As shown in
\cref{prop:relative-product-obstruction}, each one-point extension
$\mathbb N\hookrightarrow\mathbb N\sqcup\{\star\}$ has finite outside
complement, whereas the diagonal copy of $\mathbb N$ in the product of two such
extensions has infinitely many mixed outside points $(a,\star)$.  Consequently,
the finite-family theorem here is obtained by a direct relative finite-pattern
construction rather than by product closure of pairwise separators.  Abstract finite-set discrimination is classical; what changes here is the
hypothesis class in which it is achieved.

\subsection{Filtered, profinite, and nominal-profinite completions}
Cauchy completion of separated filtrations and factorial synchronization of
finite deterministic dynamics are standard.  Profinite completions organize
observations by finite quotients or finite recognizing algebras
\cite{DaveyGouveiaHaviarPriestley2011,ChenAdamekMiliusUrbat2016,Moreau2024}.
In such settings it is also standard that finite candidates can be separated by
finite recognizers while a Cauchy family converges to a genuinely profinite
point.  The abstract topological pattern --- candidate-specific separation together
with a new completion point --- is classical.  The difference is where the
finiteness sits: profinite stages take finite quotients, whereas here the
quotient is trivial and only the complement over a retained infinite base is
bounded.

There are also established extensions of profinite methods to infinite data
alphabets.  In particular, nominal topology replaces ordinary finiteness by
orbit-finiteness and finite-support symmetry, yielding pro-orbit-finite spaces
for data languages \cite{BirkmannMiliusUrbat2023}.  This is a closer analogy to
Resolution than ordinary finite quotients, but the finiteness principle is
different.  Resolution observers preserve every base element individually and
pointwise; no quotient by renaming symmetry is taken.  Only the complement over
that entire fixed base is finitely budgeted.

That distinction is formally visible in the escape witness.  When the base is
not finitely codable, the candidate-tailored finite-pattern observer itself
cannot be encoded into any finite carrier, even though its external complement
is finite.  The same observer nevertheless separates the chosen candidate from
the whole sufficiently late syntactic tail.  Thus the Resolution-specific
content of finite-pattern escape is the relative realization of the anti-limit
witness over a fully retained infinite base, not anti-limit separation in the
abstract.

Resolution stages also differ from ordinary profinite stages because the
preserved base may be infinite.  The compression theorem deliberately isolates
the standard dynamic ingredient: only the trajectory relevant to the chosen
unary family needs a finite code.  The structural interaction used here is
between that trajectory-level compression and the relative finite-pattern
escape observer.

\subsection{Finite complements and Rees-index analogies}
Finite-complement extensions and residual finiteness are classical in semigroup
theory \cite{CainMaltcev2013,RuskucThomas1998}.  The analogy is useful but not
literal here.  In a genuinely partial Resolution algebra, the embedded old
carrier is not closed under the generated total operations; by
\cref{prop:old-image-closed}, closure occurs exactly when the evaluator was
already total.  Hence the observer class is better described as relative to a
pointwise-fixed partial base than as an ordinary finite-Rees-index extension.

\subsection{Tree metrics}
Classical infinite-tree completions are based on prefix depth
\cite{ArnoldNivat1980,Courcelle1983}.  Resolution uses semantic finite-tag
observation instead.  The formal comb counterexample in \cref{sec:completion}
shows that the two Cauchy notions differ: a sequence may be depth-Cauchy while a
fixed low-budget Resolution observer continues to distinguish adjacent terms.

\subsection{Contribution and scope}
The contribution claimed by this paper is concentrated in the following
package:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item an intrinsic finite-complement observer class over a pointwise-fixed,
        possibly infinite partial base, together with a direct finite-pattern
        realization theorem inside a class that is not product-closed over
        infinite bases;
  \item the induced observational filtration and its trajectory-level
        compression theorem;
  \item a relative finite-pattern escape mechanism whose witness may be
        globally infinite while retaining only finite external complexity, and
        the resulting compression--escape properness theorem;
  \item the exact finite-base criterion, infinite-base old-fixing instance,
        sharper unbounded-rank witnesses, and exact equational conservativity.
\end{enumerate}
Factorial periodicity, abstract finite-set discrimination, candidate-specific
anti-limit separation in profinite settings, selected-subterm sink
constructions, and the many-sorted encoding are all classical, and are used
here as such.
