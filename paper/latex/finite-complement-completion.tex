\section{The finite-complement uniformity and its completion}
\label{sec:completion}

For $m\ge1$, define a relation on $\Free D$ by
\[
 x\fceq{m}y
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \pi_T(x)=\pi_T(y)
 \text{ for every finite-complement completion }T
 \text{ with }|T\setminus A|\le m.
\]
Each $\fceq{m}$ is a congruence, being an intersection of kernels of
homomorphisms, and
\[
 \fceq{m+1}\;\subseteq\;\fceq{m}.
\]
By \cref{thm:finite-external-separation}, their intersection is equality.
Thus the congruences form a separated countable uniformity on $\Free D$.

A sequence $(x_j)$ is Cauchy if for every $m$ there is $N$ such that
$x_i\fceq{m}x_j$ whenever $i,j\ge N$.  We write
$\FCcomp{\Free D}$ for the corresponding Cauchy completion.  The canonical map
$\Free D\to\FCcomp{\Free D}$ is injective because the uniformity is separated.
We call the completion \emph{proper} when this map is not surjective.

The proofs of properness below use one elementary finite-dynamics lemma.

\begin{lemma}[Factorial synchronization]\label{lem:factorial-synchronization}
Let $s_0,s_1,\ldots$ be an orbit of a self-map on a set.  If the orbit takes at
most $N$ distinct values, then the subsequence
\[
 s_{0!},s_{1!},s_{2!},\ldots
\]
is eventually constant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Among $s_0,\ldots,s_N$ two values coincide, say $s_i=s_j$ with $i<j$.
Determinism implies that the orbit is periodic from time $i$ onward with period
$p=j-i\le N$.  Every sufficiently large factorial is divisible by $p$, so all
sufficiently late factorial indices are congruent modulo $p$ after the
preperiod.  Their orbit values are therefore equal.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Finite base}

Assume first that $A$ is finite and that some application is undefined in $D$.
Choose $f\in\Sigma$ and $a,b\in A$ with $\delta_f(a,b)$ undefined.  Put
\[
 c_0=f(a,b),\qquad c_{k+1}=f(c_k,b),
\]
where the expressions are taken in $\Free D$.  If necessary one may replace
$f(-,b)$ by any fixed one-hole context whose successive applications remain
non-base normal terms; the only property used below is strict growth of the
chosen terms.

Fix $m$.  Every completion occurring in the definition of $\fceq{m}$ has at
most $|A|+m$ elements.  Hence the image of the orbit $(c_k)$ in each such
completion takes at most $|A|+m$ values.  By
\cref{lem:factorial-synchronization}, sufficiently late factorial samples have
the same image in every completion of that size.  Therefore a suitable
factorial subsequence is Cauchy for every $m$.

On the other hand, no fixed $x\in\Free D$ can be its limit.  Choose a term
$c_N$ whose subterm set contains a longer segment of the growing orbit than
occurs in $x$.  Apply the construction of
\cref{thm:finite-pattern-realization} to the finite set $\{x,c_N\}$.  The
resulting finite-complement completion represents all selected subterms
faithfully, while the next unselected continuation is sent to the sink.  After
choosing $N$ beyond the size of $x$, this separates $x$ from all sufficiently
late members of the chosen subsequence.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-base properness]\label{thm:finite-base-properness}
If $A$ is finite, then
\[
 \FCcomp{\Free D}=\Free D
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 D\text{ is total}.
\]
Equivalently, the finite-complement completion is proper exactly when $D$ is
genuinely partial.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $D$ is partial, the preceding construction gives a Cauchy sequence with no
limit in $\Free D$, so the completion is proper.  If $D$ is total, every normal
term reduces to an element of $A$, hence $\Free D=A$.  Since $A$ is finite and
the uniformity is separated, every Cauchy sequence is eventually constant.
Thus no new completion point is added.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A sufficient criterion for an arbitrary base}

When $A$ is infinite, a finite-complement completion need not be finite, so the
preceding cardinality argument no longer applies.  It is enough, however, that
the particular orbit under consideration cannot use the base as additional
memory.

Suppose there are $g,u\in\Sigma$ and $a,b,e\in A$ such that
\[
 \delta_g(a,b)\text{ is undefined},
 \qquad
 \delta_u(x,e)=x\quad\text{for every }x\in A.
\]
Let
\[
 c_0=g(a,b),\qquad c_{k+1}=u(c_k,e)
\]
in $\Free D$.  In any finite-complement completion with at most $m$ new
elements, if the interpreted orbit ever enters $A$ then the identity
$u(x,e)=x$ makes it constant.  Otherwise it remains among at most $m$ new
elements.  Hence its nonconstant part has at most $m$ states, and
\cref{lem:factorial-synchronization} again gives a Cauchy factorial subsequence.
The same finite-subterm construction as above excludes every element of
$\Free D$ as a possible limit.

\begin{theorem}[Base-fixing context criterion]
\label{thm:base-fixing-properness}
Under the preceding hypotheses, $\FCcomp{\Free D}$ is proper.
\end{theorem}

The condition is sufficient, not necessary.  Its role is simply to exhibit a
class of infinite-base examples in which finite-complement observations still
force a new Cauchy point.
