\section{Observational Filtration, Compression, and Escape}
\label{sec:completion}

The stage relations $\stageeq{n}$ form a descending filtration: agreement at a
larger finite-tag budget implies agreement at every smaller budget.  By
\cref{thm:finite-external-separation}, their intersection is equality, so the
filtered space is separated.  A sequence is Cauchy when, for each fixed $n$,
its tail is pairwise $\stageeq{n}$-equivalent.  Quotienting Cauchy sequences by
eventual agreement at every stage gives the observational completion
$\Comp{\RAlg{D}}$.  The constant-sequence map is injective and stage-dense, and
standard diagonalization gives completeness.

The central question is when this completion contains genuinely new points.
The proof is best understood by separating two logically independent
mechanisms.

\subsection{Trajectory compression}

Fix a generated unary family $t_0,t_1,\ldots$.  In a stage-$n$ observer, suppose
its interpretation is the orbit of one deterministic self-map
\[
 s_{k+1}=F_n(s_k).
\]
We do not require the entire observer carrier to be finite.  It is enough that
the single trajectory $(s_k)$ admit a finite code with bound $N(n)$ such that,
for ordered times $i\le j$, equality of codes implies $s_i=s_j$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite trajectory compression implies factorial Cauchy]
\label{thm:trajectory-compression}
If such a finite trajectory code exists uniformly at every stage, then the
factorially sampled generated sequence
\[
 t_{0!},t_{1!},t_{2!},\ldots
\]
is Cauchy for the finite-tag filtration.
\end{theorem}

The proof is standard finite dynamics.  Among the first $N(n)+1$ coded orbit
positions some state repeats.  The resulting eventual period is at most
$N(n)$ and therefore divides $N(n)!$.  Sufficiently late factorial indices are
multiples of this period, so all late factorial samples agree at stage $n$.

The distinction between trajectory finiteness and carrier finiteness is
essential.  For finite bases the whole stage observer can be coded finitely.  In
the infinite-base old-fixing case the observer carrier remains infinite, but
the relevant orbit admits a finite quotient after collapsing the pointwise
fixed old states to a settled class.  Thus both cases instantiate the same
dynamic theorem.

\subsection{Finite-pattern escape}

Compression explains Cauchy behavior but cannot by itself imply that the
completion is proper: a Cauchy sequence might converge to a generated Answer.
The anti-limit argument comes from the concrete finite-pattern realization
mechanism.

Assume the generated family has a growing unary syntactic form
\[
 t_{k+1}=\susp(u,t_k,\old(e))
\]
for a fixed operation symbol $u$ and fixed right base argument $e$.  Every step
adds a new suspended constructor, so constructor size strictly increases.

Fix a candidate generated Answer $x$.  Choose an orbit prefix $t_N$ longer
than $x$ and build the finite-pattern observer from the two roots $x$ and
$t_N$.  The observer evaluates every selected subterm to its exact injective
encoding.  The next orbit node $t_{N+1}$ is too large to be a subterm of either
selected root.  Hence the transition from the encoded prefix to the fixed right
argument misses the selected lookup table and is sent to $\overflow$.  No
selected node has overflow as an encoded left child, so the same unary context
keeps overflow fixed thereafter.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-pattern escape]
\label{thm:finite-pattern-escape}
For every candidate $x\in\RAlg{D}$, a candidate-dependent finite-tag observer
separates $x$ from every sufficiently late term of the factorially sampled
growing unary orbit.  Consequently the factorial sample has no generated
limit.
\end{theorem}

Unlike \cref{thm:trajectory-compression}, this proof does not use finite-state
periodicity.  Its input is the finite-pattern observer together with syntactic
growth and absorbing overflow.

\subsection{Combined properness theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Compression--escape properness]
\label{thm:compression-escape-properness}
Suppose a generated unary family satisfies both:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item at each finite observation stage its interpreted trajectory admits a
        uniformly finite trajectory code; and
  \item its generated syntax grows by repeated application of a fixed unary
        context of the form $t\mapsto\susp(u,t,\old(e))$.
\end{enumerate}
Then the factorial sample is Cauchy and has no generated limit.  Therefore the
generated filtered space is incomplete and the canonical embedding
\[
 \RAlg{D}\longrightarrow\Comp{\RAlg{D}}
\]
is not surjective.
\end{theorem}


This is the structural center of the properness theory.  Compression and escape
are complementary rather than redundant: the first makes late terms
observationally indistinguishable at each fixed stage, while the second shows
that no finite generated Answer can absorb that Cauchy behavior as its limit.

\subsection{Finite-base instance}

Suppose $A$ injects into $\Fin(m)$ and some base application
$\eval_D(f,a,z)$ is undefined.  Let $c_0=\old(a)$ and iterate the unresolved
left comb $c_{k+1}=f(c_k,\old(z))$.  A stage-$n$ observer has at most
$m+n+1$ coded states, so its comb trajectory is finitely coded.  The comb also
has the growing unary syntax required by finite-pattern escape.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-base properness criterion]
\label{prop:proper-completion}
For a finitely coded base, the canonical completion embedding is surjective if
and only if the evaluator of $D$ is total.  Equivalently, the completion is
proper exactly when some base application is undefined.
\end{theorem}

The reverse direction is elementary: if the evaluator is total, every generated
Answer reduces to an embedded base value and every Cauchy sequence is eventually
constant at a separating finite stage.

\subsection{Infinite-base old-fixing instance}

No finite code of the whole base is needed when the orbit is constrained by a
context that fixes old elements pointwise.  Suppose
\[
 \eval_D(g,a,b)=\operatorname{none},
 \qquad
 \eval_D(u,x,e)=\operatorname{some}(x)\quad(x\in A),
\]
and define
\[
 c_0=\susp(g,\old(a),\old(b)),
 \qquad
 c_{k+1}=\susp(u,c_k,\old(e)).
\]
In any stage-$n$ observer, if the orbit enters the old base then the old-fixing
law makes it constant.  Otherwise it stays entirely in the $n$ tags plus
$\overflow$, hence in only $n+1$ external states.  This yields the sharp
factorial synchronization
\[
 c_{(n+1)!}\stageeq{n}c_{(n+2)!}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Old-fixing properness and unbounded rank]
\label{thm:old-fixing-proper-completion}
Under the hypotheses above, the observational completion is proper.  Moreover,
for every $n$ the pair $c_{(n+1)!},c_{(n+2)!}$ is distinct but agrees at stage
$n$, so its least finite-tag separation rank is greater than $n$.
\end{theorem}


The finite-base and old-fixing hypotheses are incomparable.  A one-point
partial algebra can be finitely coded while admitting no base-fixing unary
context; natural arithmetic has the context $x\mapsto x+0$ but no finite code
of its carrier.  The common theorem is not a common hypothesis on the base, but
a common mechanism on the relevant observer trajectory.

\subsection{Why the filtration is not prefix-depth observation}

The distinction from the classical infinite-tree metric is visible on an
explicit unresolved comb.  Let
\[
 b_0=\old(a),\qquad b_{k+1}=\susp(f,b_k,\old(z)),
\]
where $\eval_D(f,a,z)$ is undefined.  Prefix-depth observation forgets what
happens sufficiently far down the left spine: for every fixed depth $d$, all
$b_i,b_j$ with $i,j\ge d$ have the same prefix through depth $d$.  Thus the
comb sequence is Cauchy for the standard prefix-depth uniformity and has the
usual infinite-comb limit in the corresponding tree completion.

Finite-tag observation behaves differently.  There is one compatible stage-1
observer that uses a single fresh tag $\tau$ and overflow $\overflow$.  The
undefined base application is sent to $\tau$, and subsequent applications of
the same comb context alternate
\[
 \tau\longmapsto\overflow\longmapsto\tau\longmapsto\overflow\longmapsto\cdots.
\]
Consequently the same fixed one-tag observer distinguishes $b_k$ from
$b_{k+1}$ for every $k$, no matter how deep the disagreement lies.  The comb is
therefore not observationally Cauchy.

\begin{proposition}[Depth-Cauchy does not imply observational Cauchy]
\label{prop:depth-observation-contrast}
There is a generated comb sequence that is Cauchy for prefix-depth agreement
but not Cauchy for the finite-tag observational filtration.  Hence the two
uniformities are distinct.
\end{proposition}
