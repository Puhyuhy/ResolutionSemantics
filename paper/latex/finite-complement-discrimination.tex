\section{Discrimination by finite-complement completions}
\label{sec:finite-separation}

We now prove the main theorem.  Throughout this section, a
\emph{finite-complement completion} means a compatible total algebra $T$
containing the base $A$ pointwise and satisfying $|T\setminus A|<\infty$.
The associated homomorphism $\pi_T:\Free D\to T$ is the map from
\cref{thm:free-completion}.

For a normal term $x\in\Free D$, let $\operatorname{Sub}(x)$ be its finite set
of normal subterms.  For a finite set $X\subseteq\Free D$, put
\[
 S(X)=\bigcup_{x\in X}\operatorname{Sub}(x),
 \qquad
 N(X)=S(X)\setminus A.
\]
Thus $N(X)$ is the finite set of non-base subterms that occur in the chosen
terms.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-subset discrimination]
\label{thm:finite-pattern-realization}
For every finite subset $X\subseteq\Free D$ there is a finite-complement
completion $T_X$ such that
\[
 \pi_{T_X}|_X:X\longrightarrow T_X
\]
is injective.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose a set of new elements
\[
 Q_X=\{q_u:u\in N(X)\}\sqcup\{\ast\},
\]
all distinct and disjoint from $A$, and put
\[
 T_X=A\sqcup Q_X.
\]
We define each basic binary operation $f\in\Sigma$ on all of $T_X^2$.

First, if $a,b\in A$ and $\delta_f(a,b)=c$ is defined in $D$, set
\[
 f_{T_X}(a,b)=c.
\]
This is forced by compatibility.

Second, suppose $u=f(s,t)$ belongs to $N(X)$.  Define
\[
 \widehat{s}=
 \begin{cases}
 s,&s\in A,\\
 q_s,&s\in N(X),
 \end{cases}
 \qquad
 \widehat{t}=
 \begin{cases}
 t,&t\in A,\\
 q_t,&t\in N(X).
 \end{cases}
\]
Since $S(X)$ is closed under subterms, both $\widehat s$ and $\widehat t$ are
available in $T_X$.  Set
\[
 f_{T_X}(\widehat s,\widehat t)=q_u.
\]

These prescriptions are consistent.  The first and second clauses cannot
conflict: if $\widehat s=s\in A$ and $\widehat t=t\in A$ and
$\delta_f(s,t)$ were defined, then $f(s,t)$ would reduce to a base element and
would not belong to $N(X)$, by \cref{lem:normality}.  Two prescriptions from
the second clause cannot conflict either, because equality of the input pair
$(\widehat s,\widehat t)$ recovers $s$ and $t$, and hence recovers the normal
term $u=f(s,t)$.

For every pair not covered by the first two clauses, define
\[
 f_{T_X}(r,s)=\ast.
\]
This makes $T_X$ a total algebra.  By the first clause it is compatible with
$D$, it contains $A$ pointwise, and
\[
 |T_X\setminus A|=|N(X)|+1<\infty.
\]

It remains to see what the canonical homomorphism does on $S(X)$.  We claim
that
\[
 \pi_{T_X}(u)=
 \begin{cases}
 u,&u\in A,\\
 q_u,&u\in N(X).
 \end{cases}
\]
This follows by induction on the construction of $u$.  The base case is clear.
If $u=f(s,t)\in N(X)$, the induction hypothesis identifies the values of $s$
and $t$ with $\widehat s$ and $\widehat t$, and the second clause in the
definition of $f_{T_X}$ gives $\pi_{T_X}(u)=q_u$.

Distinct elements of $S(X)$ therefore have distinct images: base elements stay
distinct, distinct non-base terms receive distinct labels, and the two classes
are disjoint.  In particular $\pi_{T_X}$ is injective on $X$.
\end{proof}

The two-element case gives ordinary separation.

\begin{corollary}[Pairwise separation]
\label{thm:finite-external-separation}
If $x,y\in\Free D$ and $x\ne y$, then some finite-complement completion $T$
satisfies $\pi_T(x)\ne\pi_T(y)$.
Moreover one may choose
\[
 |T\setminus A|\le |N(\{x,y\})|+1.
\]
\end{corollary}

The theorem is a discrimination statement in the usual universal-algebraic
sense: one homomorphism is required to be injective on a prescribed finite
subset.  The qualification \emph{finite-complement} refers to the restricted
class of codomains, not to a new notion of discrimination.

\subsection{Why the usual product argument leaves the class}

For classes of algebras closed under finite products, finite-set discrimination
can often be obtained from pairwise separation by taking the product of
finitely many separating homomorphisms.  That argument is not available here
when the retained base is infinite.

\begin{proposition}[Failure of product closure]
\label{prop:relative-product-obstruction}
Let $A$ be infinite.  The class of set embeddings $A\hookrightarrow T$ with
finite complement $T\setminus A$ is not closed under binary products over the
diagonal copy of $A$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take $T=A\sqcup\{\ast\}$.  The inclusion $A\hookrightarrow T$ has one-element
complement.  In $T^2$, embed $A$ diagonally by $a\mapsto(a,a)$.  For every
$a\in A$, the point $(a,\ast)$ lies outside the diagonal image, and these points
are pairwise distinct.  Hence the complement of the diagonal copy of $A$ in
$T^2$ is infinite.
\end{proof}

This proposition does not by itself establish novelty; it only explains why
the direct proof of \cref{thm:finite-pattern-realization} is not replaced by
the standard finite-product proof within the same restricted class.

\subsection{Finite bases}

If $A$ is finite, every finite-complement completion is finite.  Hence
\cref{thm:finite-pattern-realization} implies the familiar residual-finiteness
consequence.

\begin{corollary}
If $A$ is finite, then $\Free D$ is residually finite.
\end{corollary}

For infinite $A$, the completions constructed above are generally infinite;
what remains finite is only the number of elements added beyond the fixed base.
