\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Partial algebras distinguish defined applications from undefined ones.  A
standard way to totalize such a structure is to pass to a free compatible
completion, retaining enough syntax to represent unresolved applications.  In
the one-sorted binary setting used here, an expression is resolved bottom-up:
applications already defined in the base algebra reduce to embedded base
values, while unresolved applications remain finite suspended trees.  The
resulting generated Answer algebra is a concrete presentation of a free
compatible extension.  Free completion itself is established theory, and we do
not claim novelty for that substrate; see the Schmidt-construction literature
and the many-sorted partial-to-total encoding of Diaconescu
\cite{ClimentVidalCosmeLlopez2023,Diaconescu2009}.

The paper studies a more specific relative observation problem.  Let $D$ be a
binary partial algebra with carrier $A$.  We consider compatible total
extensions whose embedding of $A$ is injective and whose complement over that
pointwise-preserved copy of $A$ is finite.  Crucially, $A$ itself need not be
finite.  Thus the observation budget measures only genuinely new semantic
states beyond the original partial base.

This relative observer class gives rise to two structural mechanisms.

\paragraph{Finite-pattern realization.}
For every finite list of generated Answers there is one compatible
finite-complement observer that is simultaneously injective on the whole list.
The observer retains the full base pointwise, assigns finite tags to the
selected suspended subterms, and sends unselected behavior to one overflow
state.  Pairwise separation is therefore not the primitive phenomenon: it is
the two-point instance of a finite-pattern theorem.  This is the first master
principle of the paper.

\paragraph{Compression and escape.}
The finite observers induce a separated filtration and a Cauchy completion.
To understand when that completion adds new points, we separate two independent
mechanisms.  First, if a chosen generated unary family is interpreted at every
finite stage as a deterministic trajectory with uniformly finite trajectory
complexity, then the factorially sampled family is Cauchy.  This uses only
pigeonhole periodicity and factorial divisibility; it is standard finite-state
dynamics and is not claimed as new.  Second, a syntactically growing unary
orbit has a Resolution-specific anti-limit property.  Given a proposed finite
Answer $x$, the finite-pattern construction can retain $x$ and a sufficiently
long orbit prefix exactly.  The next unseen iterate cannot belong to the
selected finite pattern, so it is sent to overflow; overflow is absorbing along
the continuation.  Hence every sufficiently late iterate is separated from
$x$.  This is the finite-pattern escape mechanism.

The combination is the conceptual center of the paper:
\[
  \boxed{
  \text{finite trajectory compression}
  +\text{ finite-pattern escape}
  \Longrightarrow
  \text{proper observational completion}.}
\]
The two halves play opposite roles.  Compression makes the factorial sample
indistinguishable at every fixed finite stage, while escape prevents it from
converging to any finite generated Answer.  Their interaction forces a new
completion point.

This organization unifies results that can otherwise look unrelated.  When the
base is finitely coded, the whole stage observer is finite, so trajectory
compression is automatic and one obtains an exact criterion: the completion is
proper exactly when the base evaluator is not total.  For an arbitrary, possibly
infinite base, a unary context that fixes every base element pointwise still
compresses the relevant orbit even though the ambient observer carrier may be
infinite.  The same finite-pattern escape argument supplies nonconvergence.
Thus finite-base and old-fixing properness are instances of one combined
mechanism, not two independent completion tricks.

The old-fixing analysis also yields quantitative information.  At stage $n$
there are only $n+1$ external states---the $n$ tags plus overflow---unless the
orbit enters the pointwise-fixed base, after which it stabilizes.  Consequently
\[
  c_{(n+1)!}\stageeq{n}c_{(n+2)!},
\]
for a distinct pair of iterates, and the least finite-tag separation ranks are
unbounded.  The factorial indices are only a synchronization device for finite
periods; the mathematical content lies in the relative observer structure that
makes such finite compression coexist with finite-pattern escape.

The completion also preserves exactly the universal equations in the language
with named base constants.  Natural and integer arithmetic provide concrete
infinite-base examples: division by zero remains a structured unresolved
Answer, while the context $x\mapsto x+0$ produces the compressed escaping orbit
that witnesses a proper completion.

To locate the construction precisely within established partial-algebra theory,
we additionally give a Lean-checked many-sorted implementation of the
operations-only fragment of Diaconescu's encoding.  It supports arbitrary
finite arities and separate total and partial operation families, proves the
$\Gamma$--$\alpha$--$\beta$--$\gamma$ satisfaction and adjunction results used
here, and canonically identifies its singleton-sort binary specialization with
the original Resolution implementation.  This bridge is supporting provenance,
not a third novelty claim.

\subsection{Main results and paper organization}

The logical dependency of the paper is as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \Cref{sec:free-completion} records the normalized free compatible
        completion used as the substrate.
  \item \Cref{sec:finite-separation} develops intrinsic finite-complement
        observers and proves relative finite-pattern realization.
  \item \Cref{sec:completion} constructs the observational filtration and
        isolates trajectory compression, finite-pattern escape, and their
        combined properness theorem.  The finite-base and old-fixing criteria
        follow as instances.
  \item \Cref{sec:equations,sec:arithmetic} give exact equational
        conservativity and arithmetic examples.
  \item \Cref{sec:diaconescu-bridge} verifies the many-sorted encoding bridge.
  \item \Cref{sec:related-work,sec:limitations} delimit the contribution and
        remaining open problems.
\end{enumerate}

The paper deliberately distinguishes standard machinery from the
Resolution-specific statements.  Free compatible completion, ordinary
finite-state periodicity, factorial synchronization, and generic Cauchy
completion are background mechanisms.  The contribution claimed here is the
relative observer framework over a pointwise-preserved partial base, its
finite-pattern realization theorem, and the way finite-pattern escape interacts
with trajectory-level compression to force proper observational completions,
including over infinite bases.
