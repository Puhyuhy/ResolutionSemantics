\section{Conclusion}

Finite-complement compatible extensions give a natural congruence topology on
the free compatible extension $F(D)$ of a partial algebra.  The elementary
finite-subset separation statement needed to make this topology Hausdorff is a
Rees-congruence construction on a finite subterm closure; it belongs to standard
finite-state algebraic technology rather than to the main contribution.

For a finite base, the topology is exactly the ordinary profinite topology.
The distinct situation occurs when the retained base is infinite, since the
allowed target algebras may then be infinite even though only finitely many
points lie outside the base.

The main result gives one sufficient condition for incompleteness in this
infinite-base setting.  An undefined base application supplies a non-base term,
and a nontrivial unary context acting identically on the base constrains its
orbit in every finite-complement extension to finitely many effective states.
A common factorial subsequence is therefore Cauchy across all extensions of a
fixed complement bound.  A Rees quotient adapted to any proposed term limit
then separates that term from the late orbit.  The Hausdorff completion must
therefore contain a point not represented by a finite normal term.

The criterion is not claimed to be necessary.  A natural next question is to
characterize incompleteness of the finite-complement congruence topology over
arbitrary infinite bases without first exhibiting a base-fixing context.
