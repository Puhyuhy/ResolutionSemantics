\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Let $D=(A,\Sigma,\delta)$ be a one-sorted partial algebra with finite binary
signature.  We write $F(D)$ for the standard free compatible total extension of
$D$: applications already defined on the base are evaluated, while undefined
applications remain as normal terms.  Free completion of partial algebras is
classical and will be used only as background
\cite{ClimentVidalCosmeLlopez2023,Diaconescu2009}.

We study compatible total extensions $T$ that retain the whole base $A$
pointwise and add only finitely many elements.  Thus
\[
 A\subseteq T,
 \qquad |T\setminus A|<\infty,
\]
and every operation value already defined in $D$ has the same value in $T$.
The universal property of $F(D)$ gives a canonical homomorphism
\[
 \pi_T:F(D)\longrightarrow T
\]
fixing $A$ pointwise.  We call such a $T$ a \emph{finite-complement compatible
extension}.

The first observation is elementary.  If $X\subseteq F(D)$ is finite, then one
can retain the finite subterm closure of $X$ and collapse everything else into
one class.  The complement is an ideal of the term algebra, so this is a Rees
congruence in the usual universal-algebraic sense \cite{Tichy1981}.  The
resulting quotient is a finite-complement compatible extension and the quotient
map is injective on $X$.  This is the algebraic version of the standard
finite-subterm plus sink construction used in finite tree automata
\cite{ComonEtAl2008}.  We record it because it supplies the separating
congruences used below, not because the construction itself is new.

For $m\ge1$, let $\theta_m$ be the intersection of the kernels of all canonical
maps into finite-complement compatible extensions satisfying
$|T\setminus A|\le m$.  The preceding separation observation implies
\[
 \bigcap_{m\ge1}\theta_m=\Delta_{F(D)}.
\]
Thus $(\theta_m)$ defines a Hausdorff congruence topology on $F(D)$, in the
standard sense of congruence topologies on universal algebras
\cite{BulmanFleming1971}.  We consider its Hausdorff completion.

The finite-base case is not a new completion theory.  When $A$ is finite, the
finite-complement quotients that fix $A$ are cofinal among all finite quotients
of $F(D)$.  Consequently the topology above is exactly the ordinary profinite
topology; we use standard profinite terminology in that case
\cite{DaveyGouveiaHaviarPriestley2011,ChenAdamekMiliusUrbat2016}.  This reduces
the genuinely different case to an infinite retained base, where the allowed
target algebras may themselves be infinite and the congruences $\theta_m$ need
not have finite index.

Our main result gives a sufficient condition for the completion to be strictly
larger than $F(D)$ in that infinite-base setting.  Suppose $D$ has an undefined
base application, producing a non-base normal term $c_0$, and suppose there is
a nontrivial one-hole unary context $C[-]$ such that
\[
 C^D(a)=a\qquad(a\in A).
\]
Iterate the context in the free completion:
\[
 c_{n+1}=C[c_n].
\]
In every extension with at most $m$ new elements, this orbit either enters the
base and then stays fixed, or remains among at most $m$ non-base elements.
Hence its factorially indexed subsequence is eventually constant in every such
extension, uniformly in the choice of extension, and is therefore Cauchy for
all $\theta_m$.

It does not converge to a term of $F(D)$.  For any proposed term $x$, retain the
finite subterm closure of $x$ together with a sufficiently long initial orbit
segment and collapse the complement.  The next iterate lies in the collapsed
ideal, and all later iterates remain there, while $x$ is retained as a
singleton class.  Hence the same quotient separates $x$ from the entire late
tail.  The Hausdorff completion therefore contains a new point.

The finite-complement condition is related in spirit to finite-index and
separability conditions studied elsewhere, for example in semigroup theory
\cite{RuskucThomas1998,CainMaltcev2013,MillerOReillyQuickRuskuc2022}.  The
present setting is different in that the retained carrier is only a partial
base and need not be a subalgebra of $F(D)$.  The paper therefore makes no
claim that the finite-complement condition itself is a new finiteness notion.

The paper is organized accordingly.  \Cref{sec:free-completion} recalls the free
compatible extension.  \Cref{sec:finite-separation} gives the short Rees
congruence separation lemma.  \Cref{sec:completion} defines the congruence
topology, identifies the finite-base case with the profinite topology, and
proves the infinite-base criterion.  \Cref{sec:examples} gives one arithmetic
example.

A machine-checked development of the underlying results is available separately
from the paper; none of the arguments below depends on it.
