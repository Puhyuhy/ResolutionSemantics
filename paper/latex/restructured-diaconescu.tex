\section{Localization inside the Many-Sorted Encoding}
\label{sec:diaconescu-bridge}

The main separation and completion theorems above are one-sorted and binary.
Their free-completion substrate sits inside a broader partial-to-total
theory, and it is worth saying exactly where.  The purpose of this section is
to identify the present construction as a specialization of the standard
many-sorted encoding of partial algebras into total ones
\cite{Diaconescu2009}, so that no part of the substrate is presented as
independent of that theory.  The statement to be recorded is a localization:
the one-sorted binary construction of \cref{sec:free-completion} is
canonically the singleton-sort, no-$TF$, binary-$PF$ case of that encoding.

A many-sorted signature has sorts $S$, designated-total operation symbols
$TF$, and partial operation symbols $PF$, each with arbitrary finite arity and
typed argument and result sorts.  Encoded total models extend the data carriers
with an auxiliary truth carrier and existence-equality operations.  The Horn
conditions $\Gamma$ ensure that the defined elements form the partial reduct
$\beta(M)$.

The translation $\alpha$ sends existence-equality atoms to equality with the
truth constant and guards universal quantification by definedness. Lean
proves the term soundness and completeness lemmas and hence the satisfaction
condition \[ M\models\alpha(\rho) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\beta(M)\models\rho.

For every partial algebra $D$, the canonical model $\gamma(D)$ uses
normalized formal applications as its total carrier. The universal lift from
a partial homomorphism into a reduct is formalized as a hom-set equivalence;
identity, composition, naturality, unit/counit behavior, and both triangle
identities are checked.

At fixed signature and universe bound, the development proves the initiality
transfers needed for the operations-only quasi-existence theories considered
here. A conditional term-model construction supplies the initial translated
encoded model, and the $\beta$ reduct yields the corresponding initial
partial model. Semantic consequence is preserved and reflected by the
translation.

Finally, when the signature has one sort, no designated-total symbols, and
only binary partial symbols, Lean constructs canonical equivalences between
the many-sorted generated carrier and the original binary Resolution
carrier, and between the corresponding encoded models.

\begin{remark}[What this section does and does not do]
The content here is a placement result.  The encoding, the adjunction and the
existence of free partial completions are due to \citet{Diaconescu2009} and are
used as established theory; the only statement added is that the binary
implementation of \cref{sec:free-completion} is the singleton-sort case of that
encoding.  The observer theory of \cref{sec:finite-separation,sec:completion}
is \emph{not} lifted to the many-sorted setting here, and doing so is open.
\end{remark}
