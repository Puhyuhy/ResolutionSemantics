\title{\textbf{Resolution Semantics for Partial Algebras}\\
A Lean-Checked Many-Sorted Encoding Bridge and a Binary Observational Completion}
\author{Adrian Puha}
\date{17 August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Starting from Diaconescu's total-algebra encoding of partial algebras, we give
a concrete Lean-checked bridge for many-sorted signatures with arbitrary
finite arities and disjoint total and partial operation families.  The bridge
implements the Horn theory $\Gamma$, the model reduct $\beta$, the sentence
translation $\alpha$, and a canonical free model $\gamma$.  It checks the two
term-evaluation lemmas, the $\alpha$--$\beta$ satisfaction condition, recovery
of the source partial algebra up to canonical equivalence, and the unique-lift
property of $\gamma$.  We also check the functor laws, package the lift as a
hom-set equivalence natural in both variables, construct its unit and counit,
verify both triangle identities, and prove both fixed-signature initiality
transfers.  In particular, the $\beta$ reduct of an initial encoded model is
initial on the partial side.  For an explicit syntax of quasi-existence
equations, we additionally construct the initial conditional term model of
$\Gamma\cup\alpha(E)$ and obtain an initial partial model of $E$.  This checks
the fixed-signature, operations-only initial-model conclusion used in
Diaconescu's Corollary~4.2.  We also derive preservation and reflection of semantic
consequence for arbitrary theories of closed formulas.  This part is positioned as a machine-checked
continuation in a concrete Lean representation, not as a novelty claim for
the underlying encoding.  For the singleton-sort signature with no designated
total symbols and only binary partial symbols, we additionally prove that this
many-sorted construction is formally equivalent to the pre-existing binary
Resolution implementation.  The equivalence covers the generated data and
truth carriers and commutes with the totalized operations, existence equality,
and the distinguished truth value.

In the binary Resolution layer, we formalize a normalized free compatible completion of binary partial
algebras.  Old-defined applications reduce to old values; old-undefined
applications become structured finite-tree Answers.  The Answer algebra has
the expected universal property and is the expression algebra modulo equality
after resolution.

Our additional construction is an intrinsic finite-complement separating
semantics.  Every distinct pair is distinguished by a compatible total
extension that fixes the old carrier pointwise and whose complement injects
into $\Fin(n+1)$.  At each budget, pair separation by this intrinsic class is
equivalent to pair separation by the canonical finite-tag models
\[
  D.\mathrm{Carrier}\;\sqcup\;\Fin(n)\;\sqcup\;\{\overflow\}.
\]
The fresh-tag count is at most the combined constructor size; this is a
construction bound, not an optimality claim.
When the old carrier is finitely coded, the same witnesses are finite in the
ordinary sense, so the generated Answer algebra is residually finite with an
explicit total-state bound.  For a finite operation signature, the qualitative
residual-finiteness conclusion also follows from classical recognizability of
finite ground-equation congruence classes; the construction here additionally
keeps the old carrier faithful and supplies the displayed bound.

Finite-tag agreement induces a separated filtration whose Cauchy completion
supports canonical operations and has exactly the same universal equations in
the language with named old-carrier constants.
For a finitely coded old carrier, the embedding is surjective exactly when the
old evaluator is total.  Without any finiteness assumption, an undefined old
application plus a context $x\mapsto u(x,e)$ fixing every old element yields a
factorial Cauchy orbit with no generated limit.  Every finite stage is strictly
coarser than equality, and least separating budgets are unbounded.  Natural
and integer arithmetic satisfy this criterion using $0/0$ and $x\mapsto x+0$,
so both completion embeddings are proper.  Division by zero remains a
structured non-old Answer, not an ordinary number.  Lean~4.33.0 checks the
development.  The exact priority of this combined package remains under
specialist review.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

The paper has two deliberately separated scopes.  First, it connects the
Resolution normal-form construction directly to Diaconescu's many-sorted
encoding of partial algebras as total algebras \cite{Diaconescu2009}.  This
bridge allows arbitrary finite operation arities and distinguishes designated
total symbols from partial symbols.  Second, the separation, filtration,
completion, properness, and arithmetic developments specialize to the
one-sorted binary setting used by the original Resolution project.  Claims
below state explicitly which of these two scopes is in force.  On their exact
overlap, a separate Lean module identifies the singleton-sort, no-$TF$,
binary-$PF$ instance of the first scope with the original binary
implementation used by the second.

Let $D$ be a one-sorted binary partial algebra with carrier $A$, operation
symbols $\Sigma$, and evaluator
\[
  \eval_D : \Sigma \longrightarrow A \longrightarrow A
    \longrightarrow \operatorname{Option}(A).
\]
The value $\operatorname{some}(c)$ records that the old application is defined
and equals $c$; $\operatorname{none}$ records old undefinedness.
We call $A$ \emph{finitely coded} when it admits an injection into $\Fin(m)$
for some $m$.  This is classically equivalent to finiteness and is the exact
hypothesis used by the Lean statements below.

There is extensive prior work on completing or encoding partial algebras.
Climent Vidal and Cosme Ll\'opez prove that the inclusion of total algebras into
partial algebras has a left adjoint, the absolutely free completion functor,
and develop the functorial Schmidt construction
\cite{ClimentVidalCosmeLlopez2023}.  Diaconescu gives the Horn-style encoding
of partial algebras as total algebras on which our first bridge is based
\cite{Diaconescu2009}.  Completion questions
also occur in partial combinatory algebra and term-rewriting models
\cite{BethkeKlopDeVrijer1996,BethkeKlopDeVrijer1999,Nesin2011}.  Accordingly,
this paper does not claim that the existence of a free or total extension is
new.  Classical tree-automata results also imply ordinary residual finiteness
for the finite-base, finite-signature ground-equational presentation discussed
below \cite{Kozen1977,ComonEtAl2008,ClimentVidalCosmeLlopez2020}; that
qualitative special case is not claimed as new either.

Our starting presentation is operationally simple.  Resolve an expression
bottom-up.  If a node has two old operands and the old evaluator returns a
value, replace the node by that old value.  Otherwise retain the node, its
operation symbol, and its resolved children.  The resulting finite trees are
called \emph{Answers}.  This gives a normalized representative for a free
compatible total extension.

After checking that encoding bridge, the binary Resolution layer asks two
narrower questions.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(Q\arabic*)]
  \item Can equality of generated Answers be characterized by total models
        that do not themselves contain Answer trees, while preserving every
        old-defined application?
  \item Can those finite observations support a completion on which the
        operations remain canonical and all constant-bearing universal
        equations are unchanged?
\end{enumerate}

The answer to both is yes.  The paper therefore separates one bridge result
from two principal binary Resolution results.

\begin{theorem}[Many-sorted encoding bridge]
\label{thm:diaconescu-bridge}
Let $\Sigma=(S,TF,PF)$ be a many-sorted partial-algebra signature whose
operations have arbitrary finite arities.  In the concrete operations-only
Lean presentation developed here:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item the Horn clauses $\Gamma$ define a reduct $\beta$ and the translation
        $\alpha$ satisfies
        $M\models\alpha(\rho)$ if and only if
        $\beta(M)\models\rho$;
  \item every partial $\Sigma$-algebra $D$ has a canonical encoded model
        $\gamma(D)$ satisfying $\Gamma$, and $\beta(\gamma(D))$ is canonically
        equivalent to $D$ as a partial algebra;
  \item every partial homomorphism $D\to\beta(M)$ has a unique encoded lift
        $\gamma(D)\to M$ agreeing with it under that equivalence;
  \item the induced maps on homomorphisms respect identities and composition,
        the lift is an explicit hom-set equivalence natural in both variables,
        and its unit and counit satisfy both triangle identities;
  \item at a fixed signature and universe bound, $\gamma$ sends initial partial
        models to initial encoded models, while the $\beta$ reduct of an
        initial model of $\Gamma\cup\alpha(E)$ is an initial partial model of
        $E$; for every theory $E$ in the concrete syntax of quasi-existence
        equations, a semantic conditional term model supplies the required
        initial encoded model and hence an initial partial model; moreover, for arbitrary sets
        $E,E'$ of closed formulas,
        \[
          E\models_{PA}E'
          \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
          \alpha(E)\cup\Gamma\models_{ALG}\alpha(E').
        \]
  \item when the signature has one sort, no designated-total symbols, and only
        binary partial symbols, the generated carrier and the canonical
        $\gamma$ model are equivalent to the pre-existing binary Resolution
        implementation, with operations, existence equality, and the truth
        constant preserved in both directions.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Structured free completion and finite separation]
\label{thm:free-and-separation}
For every one-sorted binary partial algebra $D$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item the generated Answer algebra $\RAlg{D}$ is initial among compatible
        total algebras equipped with a map from the old carrier;
  \item $\RAlg{D}$ is represented by the quotient of the absolutely free
        expression algebra by the kernel of the resolution evaluator;
  \item every distinct $x,y\in\RAlg{D}$ is separated by a compatible total
        extension whose complement over $A$ has at most $|x|+|y|+1$ points;
        equivalently, a canonical finite-tag model needs at most
        $|x|+|y|$ fresh tags and one overflow state.  Consequently, when $A$
        is finitely coded, $\RAlg{D}$ is residually finite.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Observational completion and equation preservation]
\label{thm:completion-and-equations}
Finite-tag agreement defines a separated descending filtration on $\RAlg{D}$.
Its Cauchy completion $\Comp{\RAlg{D}}$ is complete, contains an injective
stage-dense image of $\RAlg{D}$, and carries the unique jointly
nonexpansive extensions of the primitive operations.  Relative to complete,
separated filtered compatible total extensions whose old embedding is
injective, the completed algebra has the expected universal extension
property.  If the old carrier is finitely coded, the canonical embedding is
surjective exactly when the old evaluator is total; equivalently, the
completion is proper exactly when some old application is undefined.  Without
assuming finite coding of the old carrier, suppose some old application is
undefined and
there are $u\in\Sigma$ and $e\in A$ with
$\eval_D(u,a,e)=\operatorname{some}(a)$ for every $a\in A$.  Then every finite
observation stage is strictly coarser than equality, an explicit factorial
orbit is Cauchy with no generated limit, its separation ranks are unbounded,
and the completion embedding is not surjective.  Moreover,
for every pair of constant-bearing algebraic
terms $s,t$,
\[
  \Comp{\RAlg{D}}\models s=t
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \RAlg{D}\models s=t.
\]
\end{theorem}

The first two clauses of \cref{thm:free-and-separation} place the generated
construction within established free-completion theory.  The more specific
ingredients are the intrinsic finite-complement observer class over a
pointwise-preserved base, its pairwise separator, and the observational
structure it induces.  The linear estimate is retained as a general
construction bound rather than a state-complexity advance.  Over a finitely
coded base it also gives an explicit, base-faithful bound on the entire
separating target; ordinary residual finiteness itself is classical in the
finite-signature subcase.  The finite-base
properness criterion explains exactly when the Cauchy construction adds
genuinely new points over a finitely coded old carrier; the old-fixing-context
criterion shows that properness is not confined to finite bases.  The two
hypotheses are incomparable: the one-point algebra is finitely coded and
partial but has no old-fixing context, whereas natural arithmetic has an
old-fixing context but no finite code for its carrier.  Natural and integer arithmetic
are concrete corollaries.
Theorem~\ref{thm:diaconescu-bridge} is a checked continuation of
Diaconescu's construction and is not included in the novelty claim.  We have
not found the exact separation--completion package in the literature reviewed so
far, but this is not a claim of historical priority.

\section{A Lean-Checked Bridge to Diaconescu's Encoding}
\label{sec:diaconescu-bridge}

This section records the exact connection to
\cite{Diaconescu2009}.  It is intentionally phrased as a bridge and
specialization; the underlying encoding results and their priority remain
with the cited work.  Diaconescu works with a many-sorted signature
$\Sigma=(S,TF,PF)$, where symbols in $TF$ are total and symbols in $PF$ are
partial.  The Lean structure assigns each symbol a natural-number arity, a
sort at every finite argument position, and a result sort.  Neither the set of
sorts nor either symbol family is required to be finite.

\subsection{The encoded models, $\beta$, and $\alpha$}

An encoded total model $M$ consists of a data carrier $M_s$ for each
$s\in S$, an auxiliary truth carrier $M_b$, total interpretations of all
$TF$ and $PF$ symbols on the data carriers, operations
\[
  {\mathrel{e_s}}:M_s\times M_s\longrightarrow M_b,
  \qquad \mathsf{true}\in M_b,
\]
and the Horn conditions $\Gamma$.  The implemented clauses say that total
symbols preserve and reflect definedness, partial symbols reflect
definedness, and
\[
  x\mathrel{e_s}y=\mathsf{true}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  x\mathrel{e_s}x=\mathsf{true}
  \quad\text{and}\quad x=y.
\]
Thus the carrier of the reduct is
\[
  \beta(M)_s=\{x\in M_s\mid
    x\mathrel{e_s}x=\mathsf{true}\}.
\]
Designated-total operations restrict to total operations on these subtypes;
a $PF$ symbol is defined precisely when its totalized result is marked as
defined.  Encoded homomorphisms induce partial-algebra homomorphisms between
the corresponding reducts.

The formula syntax is indexed by a list of bound-variable sorts.  Typed de
Bruijn variables make quantifier extension explicit.  It is generated by
existence-equality atoms, conjunction, negation, and one-variable universal
quantification; iterating the last constructor represents a finite sorted
variable block.  The translation sends
\[
  t\mathrel{\stackrel{e}{=}}t'
  \quad\longmapsto\quad
  (t\mathrel{e_s}t'=\mathsf{true}),
\]
commutes with the Boolean constructors, and guards a total universal
quantifier by definedness of the newly bound variable.

The two term lemmas corresponding to Lemmas~3.3 and~3.4 of
\cite{Diaconescu2009} are
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.term\_evalPartial\_sound} and
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.term\_evalPartial\_complete}.
Structural induction on formulas, with the guard handling the quantifier
case, then gives
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.alpha\_satisfaction\_condition}.
The theorem is stated for assignments and hence also yields the closed-sentence
instance in \cref{thm:diaconescu-bridge}(a).

\subsection{The canonical model $\gamma$ and its lift}

For a partial algebra $D$, raw values at each sort are either old values or
formal applications.  The normalizer treats the two symbol families
differently:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a $TF$ application whose arguments are old always reduces to the old
        total result;
  \item a $PF$ application whose arguments are old reduces exactly when the
        source partial evaluator returns a value;
  \item every remaining application is retained as a sorted formal
        suspension with its normalized arguments.
\end{itemize}
The generated subtype consists of raw values having a term witness.  Its
interpreter into any compatible total target exists and is unique.  This is
the many-sorted finite-arity free-completion argument used for the data sorts
of $\gamma(D)$.

The auxiliary carrier of $\gamma(D)$ has one true element and a failure value
for each sorted pair that is not the same diagonal old value.  Existence
equality returns true exactly on that old diagonal.  Lean checks that this
model satisfies all clauses of $\Gamma$ in
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.gamma\_satisfiesGamma}.  The old
embedding is bijective onto the defined part, and it respects both the total
and partial evaluators; this is packaged by
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.betaGammaPartialAlgEquiv}.
Finally,
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.gamma\_persistent\_liberality}
constructs the lift of every $D\to\beta(M)$ and proves uniqueness from the
free interpreter on data and the homomorphism laws on the truth carrier.

\subsection{The binary one-sorted specialization}

The many-sorted normal-form development above is implemented autonomously
from the earlier binary core.  The module
\texttt{ResolutionDiaconescuBinarySpecialization.lean} supplies the formal
comparison rather than relying on their structural resemblance.  It sends a
binary signature to a universe-polymorphic singleton data sort, an empty
family of designated-total symbols, and the original operation family as
partial symbols of arity two.  Forgetting and restoring the unique sort gives
the partial-algebra equivalence
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.BinarySpecialization.toFromPartialAlgEquiv},
and the three binary Horn clauses are shown equivalent to the specialized
many-sorted $\Gamma$ record.

At the free-completion level, the interpreters in the two directions are
constructed from the respective universal properties.  Uniqueness proves
that their composites are identities, yielding
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.BinarySpecialization.generatedEquiv}.
The equivalence preserves old elements and generated binary operations.  It
also transports the old-diagonal predicate, failure witnesses, and the
auxiliary truth carrier.  These components are assembled in
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.BinarySpecialization.gammaEquiv}, which
formally identifies the original binary $\gamma(D)$ with the binary
one-sorted reduct of the many-sorted $\gamma(D)$.  Its forward and inverse
maps commute with the operation symbols, existence equality, and the
distinguished truth value.  Thus the general layer and the binary layer use
different Lean types but agree canonically on their shared mathematical
fragment.

\subsection{The concrete adjunction, initiality, and semantic consequence}

The continuation module makes the categorical content explicit while keeping
the formal dependency surface small.  It proves the identity and composition
laws for $\beta$, packages persistent liberality as the checked bijection
\[
  \operatorname{Hom}_{ALG_\Gamma}(\gamma(D),M)
  \cong
  \operatorname{Hom}_{PA}(D,\beta(M)),
\]
and defines the induced action of $\gamma$ on homomorphisms.  The latter
respects identities and composition.  The hom-set equivalence is natural in
both variables; its unit $D\to\beta\gamma(D)$ and counit
$\gamma\beta(M)\to M$ are explicit and satisfy both triangle identities.
These statements are exposed by
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.betaHom\_comp},
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.gammaBetaHomEquiv}, and
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.gammaHom\_comp}, together with
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.gammaRestrict\_natural\_target},
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.gammaRestrict\_natural\_source},
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.gammaBetaCounit\_natural}, and
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.gammaBeta\_triangle\_gamma}.

The left-adjoint content gives a direct initial-semantics consequence.  For a
fixed signature and a fixed carrier universe closed under $\gamma$, define an
initial partial model of a theory $E$ by the usual unique-map property among
models of $E$, and define initiality on the encoded side among models of
$\Gamma$ and $\alpha(E)$.  The theorem
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.gamma\_preserves\_initiality}
proves that $\gamma$ sends the former initial model to the latter.  In the
direction used by Diaconescu's Corollary~4.2,
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.beta\_of\_initial\_encoded\_is\_initial}
proves that the $\beta$ reduct of an initial encoded model is an initial
partial model.  This isolates the categorical transfer in the published
argument.

The remaining existence step is supplied in two separate modules.  First,
\leanname{Resolution.Conditional.termModel\_initial} treats an arbitrary
many-sorted conditional-equational theory.  Sorted ground terms are quotiented
by semantic equality in every model of the theory.  Lean proves that the
resulting term algebra satisfies the theory and that evaluation induces the
unique homomorphism from it to every other model.

Second, the syntax formalized for a quasi-existence equation consists of a
finite sorted variable context, a finite list of existence-equality premises,
and one existence-equality conclusion.  Its conditional translation adds a
definedness guard for every context variable and translates each atom using
$e_s$ and $\mathsf{true}$.  The equivalence between satisfaction of this
conditional clause and satisfaction in the $\beta$ reduct is
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.InitialExistence.translateQuasiEquation\_sat\_iff\_partial}.
The five families of $\Gamma$ clauses are included in the same conditional
theory.  Applying the term-model construction to
$\Gamma\cup\alpha(E)$ gives the initial encoded model in
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.InitialExistence.initialEncodedModel\_is\_initial};
the adjunction transfer then yields
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.InitialExistence.quasiTheory\_has\_initial\_partial\_model}.
Thus the fixed-signature, same-universe, operations-only initial-model
conclusion used in Corollary~4.2 is checked end to end.  This is a
formalization of the established existence argument, not a novelty claim.

Because Lean represents $\beta(\gamma(D))$ as canonically equivalent to $D$
rather than definitionally equal to it, reflection of consequence requires an
explicit transport result.  The theorem
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.Formula.sat\_iff\_of\_equiv}
proves invariance of satisfaction under partial-algebra equivalence, including
the negation and sorted-quantifier cases.  Combining this with the
$\alpha$--$\beta$ satisfaction condition and the canonical $\gamma$ model
gives
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.semantic\_consequence\_equivalence},
the concrete Lean form of Corollary~4.3 of \cite{Diaconescu2009}.

The data carrier is exposed as the many-sorted Resolution normal-form type.
Theorems
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.gammaHom\_generatedOld},
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.gammaHom\_generatedTotal}, and
\leanname{Resolution.Diaconescu.ManySorted.gammaHom\_generatedPartial}
show that the functorial action preserves old values and the two generated
operation constructors.  This is the explicit interface between the
established encoding and the Resolution normal-form layer used here; the
specialization equivalence above connects it formally to the binary
\texttt{GeneratedAns} carrier used in the remainder of the paper.

There are two deliberate scope boundaries.  The Lean theorem uses canonical
partial-algebra equivalence rather than identifying
$\beta(\gamma(D))$ and $D$ by definitional equality.  It does not mechanize
the surrounding institution/comorphism infrastructure, arbitrary signature
morphisms, the full institution-level form of Corollary~4.2, or the
proof-theoretic Birkhoff completeness result of Section~5.  The categorical
fixed-signature transfer and the quasi-existence initial-model construction
used inside Corollary~4.2 are checked, but their packaging over arbitrary
signature morphisms is not.  These
boundaries are why the result is described here as a
many-sorted encoding bridge and not as a replacement for the broader theory
of \cite{Diaconescu2009}.

\section{Generated Answers as a Free Compatible Completion}
\label{sec:free-completion}

\subsection{Expressions and Answers}

Finite input expressions and raw Answers are defined by separate grammars:
\[
  e ::= \operatorname{val}(a)
      \mid \operatorname{app}(f,e_1,e_2),
\]
\[
  r ::= \old(a)
      \mid \susp(f,r_1,r_2).
\]
The separation is intentional.  Expressions are input syntax.  Answers are
normal forms produced by the partial evaluator together with the suspension
rule.

\begin{definition}[One-step resolution]\label{def:liftop}
For $f\in\Sigma$ and raw Answers $x,y$, let
\[
\operatorname{liftOp}_D(f,x,y)=
\begin{cases}
  \old(c),
    &x=\old(a),\ y=\old(b),\
      \eval_D(f,a,b)=\operatorname{some}(c),\\[1mm]
  \susp(f,x,y),&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The resolution function $\Res_D$ is the structural recursion determined by
\[
\Res_D(\operatorname{val}(a))=\old(a),
\]
\[
\Res_D(\operatorname{app}(f,e_1,e_2))
 =\operatorname{liftOp}_D
   \bigl(f,\Res_D(e_1),\Res_D(e_2)\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

The generated carrier is the image of this evaluator:
\[
  \RAlg{D}
  =
  \{r\mid \exists e,\ \Res_D(e)=r\}.
\]
The old embedding is $\eta_0(a)=\old(a)$.  Generated operations are
\[
  f_{\RAlg{D}}(x,y)=\operatorname{liftOp}_D(f,x,y).
\]
Generation is closed under these operations because expression witnesses can
be joined by one application node.

\begin{proposition}[Conservativity on defined applications]
\label{prop:generated-conservativity}
If $\eval_D(f,a,b)=\operatorname{some}(c)$, then
\[
  f_{\RAlg{D}}(\eta_0(a),\eta_0(b))=\eta_0(c).
\]
The map $\eta_0$ is injective.
\end{proposition}

The proposition is immediate from \cref{def:liftop}.  Its role is not depth but
control: no old-defined calculation is reinterpreted.

\subsection{Universal property}

A \emph{compatible total algebra over $D$} consists of a total $\Sigma$-algebra
$T$, a map $i:A\to T$, and the preservation law
\[
  \eval_D(f,a,b)=\operatorname{some}(c)
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  f_T(i(a),i(b))=i(c).
\]
The map $i$ is not required to be injective.  This is the target notion needed
for the ordinary free-completion universal property.

Every raw Answer has an interpretation in $T$:
\[
\Phi_T(\old(a))=i(a),
\qquad
\Phi_T(\susp(f,x,y))=f_T(\Phi_T(x),\Phi_T(y)).
\]
The preservation law gives
\[
  \Phi_T(\operatorname{liftOp}_D(f,x,y))
  =f_T(\Phi_T(x),\Phi_T(y)).
\]
The only non-structural case is the old-defined branch, where the equation is
exactly the compatibility condition.

\begin{theorem}[Free compatible completion]\label{thm:free-completion}
For every compatible total algebra $T$ over $D$, there is a unique homomorphism
\[
  \Phi_T:\RAlg{D}\longrightarrow T
\]
such that $\Phi_T\circ\eta_0=i$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Existence is the fold above.  It preserves generated operations by the
one-step equation.  For uniqueness, choose an expression $e$ generating a
given Answer.  Induction on $e$ forces every compatible homomorphism to agree
with the fold on $\Res_D(e)$.  Since every generated Answer has such a witness,
the two maps agree everywhere.
\end{proof}

The Lean-facing theorem is
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.freeCompletionUniversal}.  This result confirms
that the generated construction belongs to free-completion theory rather than
constituting a new category-theoretic notion.

\subsection{Quotient presentation}

Define an equivalence on input expressions by
\[
  e\sim_D e'
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \Res_D(e)=\Res_D(e').
\]
Then resolution descends to a map
\[
  \overline{\Res}_D:
  \operatorname{Expr}(D)/{\sim_D}\longrightarrow\RAlg{D}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Kernel quotient presentation]
\label{prop:kernel-quotient}
The map $\overline{\Res}_D$ is injective and surjective.  Thus generated
Answers are a normalized presentation of the expression algebra modulo the
resolution kernel.
\end{proposition}

The two inverse laws are formalized by
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.expressionKernelQuotientInjective} and
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.expressionKernelQuotientSurjective}.

\begin{remark}[Relation to the Schmidt construction]
The Schmidt construction and the left adjoint free-completion functor are
direct prior art \cite{ClimentVidalCosmeLlopez2023}.  Our formal theorem proves
the corresponding universal property for the concrete one-sorted binary
presentation used here.  A fully explicit categorical isomorphism between the
Lean object and every formulation of the many-sorted Schmidt construction is
not needed for the results below and is not claimed.  The correct positioning
is that $\RAlg{D}$ is a normalized free compatible completion.
\end{remark}

\section{Intrinsic Finite-Complement Separation}\label{sec:finite-separation}

The free-completion theorem alone does not show that distinctions between
suspended Answers are visible in independently specified models.  The
primitive observer condition should not depend on a particular sum encoding.

Let $T$ be a compatible total extension with injective old embedding
$i:A\hookrightarrow T$.  Define its genuine outside part by
\[
  \operatorname{Out}_D(T)
  =\{z\in T\mid \forall a\in A,\ z\ne i(a)\}.
\]

\begin{definition}[Intrinsic finite-complement observer]
An observer has budget $n$ when there is an injection
\[
  \operatorname{Out}_D(T)\hookrightarrow\Fin(n+1).
\]
Thus only the complement over the pointwise-preserved base is finite; the old
carrier itself may be infinite.  The budget is an upper bound, so no ordering,
chosen tags, or distinguished overflow point is part of this definition.
\end{definition}

Every compatible extension has the carrier decomposition
\[
  T\cong A\sqcup\operatorname{Out}_D(T).
\]
For the concrete observer used in the construction below, the outside part is
canonically $\Fin(n)\sqcup\{\overflow\}$.

\begin{definition}[Finite-tag model]
For $n\in\mathbb N$, a finite-tag model over $D$ has carrier
\[
  A\sqcup\Fin(n)\sqcup\{\overflow\}
\]
and total operations preserving every old-defined application:
\[
  \eval_D(f,a,b)=\operatorname{some}(c)
  \Longrightarrow
  f_T(\operatorname{old}(a),\operatorname{old}(b))
  =\operatorname{old}(c).
\]
No further equations are imposed.
\end{definition}

The formal representation theorem proves that intrinsic separation at budget
$n$ is equivalent to finite-tag separation at the same budget.  In the forward
direction, an intrinsic complement code yields a base-fixing injection into
the canonical carrier.  Unused tag states are allowed and are sent to overflow
when the operations are transported.  This is an injection presentation, not
a claimed bijection with all canonical states.

Write $x\stageeq{n}y$ when every finite-tag model with $n$ tags interprets $x$
and $y$ equally.  Full finite observational agreement means
\[
  x\equiv_{\mathrm{fin}}y
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \forall n,\ x\stageeq{n}y.
\]

\subsection{Pair-dependent separator}

Let $x\ne y$ be generated Answers.  Form a finite list $S(x,y)$ containing all
subterms of the two raw trees.  Repetition is harmless; first-occurrence
indices provide a finite set of tags.  Define an encoding on selected nodes:
\[
q(\old(a))=\operatorname{old}(a),
\qquad
q(s)=\operatorname{tag}(\operatorname{index}(s))
\]
for selected suspended nodes $s$.  The encoding is injective on the selected
closure.

The model operation first preserves an old-defined application.  Otherwise it
attempts to decode both inputs, constructs one suspended node, and re-encodes
that node if it belongs to the selected closure; all unrelated cases go to
$\overflow$.  There is no branch conflict.  A generated normal Answer cannot
contain a suspended node with two old children whose old application is
defined, because resolution would already have reduced it.

Induction on selected subterms gives
\[
  \Phi_T(s)=q(s).
\]
In particular, $\Phi_T(x)=q(x)$ and $\Phi_T(y)=q(y)$, so injectivity of $q$ on
$S(x,y)$ separates the roots.

\begin{theorem}[Intrinsic finite-complement separation]
\label{thm:finite-external-separation}
For distinct $x,y\in\RAlg{D}$ there exist $n$ and a compatible total extension
$T$ whose outside part injects into $\Fin(n+1)$ and such that
\[
  \Phi_T(x)\ne\Phi_T(y).
\]
One may choose
\[
  n=|x|+|y|,
\]
where $|{-}|$ is raw constructor count.  Consequently,
\[
  x\equiv_{\mathrm{fin}}y
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  x=y.
\]
\end{theorem}

The intrinsic public Lean declarations are
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.qualitativeFiniteComplementSeparating\_theorem},
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.intrinsicSeparationIffFiniteTag}, and
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.intrinsicExtensionHasCanonicalPresentation}.
The canonical finite-tag consequences remain exposed as
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.finiteExternalSeparation},
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.finiteObservationComplete}, and
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.finiteSeparationBound}.

\begin{remark}[Interpretation of the result]
The old carrier $A$ may be infinite.  The theorem is therefore not finite
residuality of the whole carrier.  It says intrinsically that a chosen pair
requires only finitely many \emph{new} semantic states beyond $A$; the
finite-tag sum is a derived normal form.  The displayed linear estimate is a
baseline construction bound, not an optimality theorem.  We avoid
using “full abstraction” as a novelty label; the mathematical content is the
explicit model class and separator.

More precisely, let $\mathcal C_D$ be the class of compatible total extensions
that fix $A$ pointwise and have finite complement over $A$.  The theorem says
that $\RAlg{D}$ is residually $\mathcal C_D$: every unequal pair is separated
by its canonical homomorphism into some member of $\mathcal C_D$.  This is an
exact classification of the statement as a relative residual property, not a
claim that the terminology or general residual paradigm is new.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-base residual finiteness]
\label{cor:finite-base-residual}
Suppose the old carrier admits an injective code into $\Fin(m)$.  Then
$\RAlg{D}$ is residually finite in the ordinary homomorphic sense.  More
precisely, distinct $x,y$ are separated by a finite compatible algebra with an
injective code into
\[
  \Fin\bigl(m+|x|+|y|+1\bigr).
\]
\end{corollary}

The formal statements are
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.ResidualComparison.finiteBaseCompatibleSeparationBound}
and
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.ResidualComparison.finiteBaseGeneratedResiduallyFinite}.
The target produced by the proof still embeds the old carrier injectively, so
the formal result is slightly stronger than ordinary residual finiteness,
whose separating homomorphisms need not be faithful on the chosen generators.

\begin{remark}[Finite-signature ground-equation comparison]
\label{rem:ground-equation-comparison}
Assume in addition that the operation signature is finite.  Regard each
$a\in A$ as a constant and let $E_D$ consist of the finitely many ground
equations
\[
  f(a,b)=c
  \qquad\text{for every }\eval_D(f,a,b)=\operatorname{some}(c).
\]
Every expression is congruent modulo $E_D$ to a term presenting its resolved
Answer.  Conversely, every equation in $E_D$ is respected by resolution.
Thus equality after resolution is exactly the congruence generated by $E_D$.

Kozen proved that the classes of a finitely generated term congruence are
recognizable tree languages \cite{Kozen1977}.  For one such class, its
finite-index syntactic congruence contains the defining congruence: congruent
terms remain congruent in every context and therefore have the same membership
in that class.  The syntactic quotient separates the chosen class from every
different one, so ordinary residual finiteness in this finite-base,
finite-signature subcase follows from that classical result.  Foundational
regular-tree systems, finite ground rewriting, standard tree-automata
treatments, and many-sorted recognizability theorems provide the broader context
\cite{Brainerd1969,DauchetTison1990,ComonEtAl2008,
ClimentVidalCosmeLlopez2020}.  We therefore make no novelty
claim for that qualitative consequence.  The explicit construction above
retains the old carrier injectively, gives the stated whole-target bound, and
continues to arbitrary old carriers by bounding only the complement; those are
the features used in the remainder of the paper.  The Lean corollary itself
does not assume that the operation signature is finite.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Closure of the old image]
\label{prop:old-image-closed}
Let $\iota_D:A\hookrightarrow\RAlg{D}$ be the canonical old embedding.  Its
image is closed under every total operation of $\RAlg{D}$ if and only if the
old evaluator is total.
\end{proposition}

Indeed, a defined old application reduces to an old value.  If
$\eval_D(f,a,b)=\operatorname{none}$, however, then
\[
  f^{\RAlg{D}}(\iota_D(a),\iota_D(b))
    =\operatorname{susp}(f,\old(a),\old(b)),
\]
which is not in the old image.  Lean checks both the pointwise escape statement
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.ResidualComparison.undefinedApplicationEscapesOldImage}
and the equivalence
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.ResidualComparison.generatedOldClosedIffEvaluatorTotal}.

The selected-subterm encoding together with an overflow state has familiar
tree-automata antecedents \cite{ComonEtAl2008}; it is not presented as a new
automaton architecture.  Its use here is constrained by compatibility with a
pointwise-preserved partial base.  The resulting targets are generally not
finite: $A$ may be infinite, while only the added tags and $\overflow$ are
counted.  A further computational analogy is the separating-words problem.
Sublinear worst-case bounds were established by
Goral{\v{c}}{\'i}k and
Koubek, Robson, and Chase \cite{GoralcikKoubek1986,Robson1989,Chase2020}.
Along a unary comb, a Resolution observer likewise iterates one transition map
from a fixed starting state.  This analogy confirms that our linear estimate is
a baseline, but it does not transfer word bounds automatically: arbitrary
Answer trees branch, and every observer must extend the pointwise-preserved
partial algebra.  The resulting relative separation complexity is open.
Determining whether this exact observer class already has a standard name
remains part of our specialist literature audit.

\section{The Observational Filtration and Completion}
\label{sec:completion}

Each $\stageeq{n}$ is an equivalence relation.  The formal model family yields
a descending filtration: agreement at a larger observational budget implies
agreement at every smaller budget.  By
\cref{thm:finite-external-separation},
\[
  \bigcap_{n\in\mathbb N}\stageeq{n}
  =\{(x,x)\mid x\in\RAlg{D}\}.
\]
Thus the filtration is separated.

A symbolic non-Archimedean distance can be read from the least separating
stage.  Equal points have distance zero; a distinct pair receives the level of
its least finite separator.  The strong triangle law follows from transitivity
of every stage relation.  This is standard filtered-space machinery, analogous
to established metric treatments of trees
\cite{ArnoldNivat1980,Courcelle1983}.

A sequence $(x_k)$ is Cauchy when, for every stage $n$, its tail is pairwise
$\stageeq{n}$-equivalent.  Two Cauchy sequences are asymptotically equivalent
when they eventually agree at every fixed stage.  Let
\[
  \Comp{\RAlg{D}}
  =
  \{\text{Cauchy sequences in }\RAlg{D}\}/\!\sim.
\]
The constant-sequence map is injective by separatedness and stage-dense by the
usual late-term argument.  A diagonal construction proves completeness.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-base properness criterion]
\label{prop:proper-completion}
Suppose the old carrier $A$ admits an injection into $\Fin(m)$ for some $m$.
Then the canonical embedding
\[
  \RAlg{D}\longrightarrow\Comp{\RAlg{D}}
\]
is surjective if and only if the old evaluator is total.  Equivalently, this
embedding is not surjective if and only if there exist $f\in\Sigma$ and
$a,z\in A$ with $\eval_D(f,a,z)=\operatorname{none}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
A stage-$n$ observer has at most $m+n+1$ states: the finitely coded old carrier,
$n$ fresh tags, and overflow.  If $\eval_D(f,a,z)$ is undefined, let $c_k$ be
the left comb obtained by iterating this application.  Along the comb family
every observer acts by the orbit of one self-map.  Hence
$(c_{k!})_{k\in\mathbb N}$ is Cauchy: sufficiently late factorial indices have
difference divisible by every possible orbit period.

For non-convergence, fix a proposed finite Answer $x$.  Choose the preserving
finite-tag observer constructed from the finite subterm closure of $x$ together
with a comb prefix longer than $x$.  It encodes $x$ and the prefix by distinct
non-overflow states.  The next comb transition matches no selected normalized
node, so it enters overflow; no selected transition has overflow as an encoded
child, making overflow absorbing along the rest of the comb.  Thus this single
finite stage separates $x$ from every sufficiently late factorial comb.  No
generated Answer is a limit.

Conversely, if the old evaluator is total, structural induction shows that
every generated Answer is old.  Cauchy agreement at a fixed separating stage
then makes every Cauchy sequence eventually constant, so the generated space
is complete.  Completeness is equivalent to surjectivity of the canonical
completion embedding.  The checked public statements are
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.FiniteBaseCompletion.completeIffTotal} and
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.FiniteBaseCompletion.embeddingNotSurjectiveIffExistsUndefined}.
\end{proof}

The one-point always-undefined algebra is the smallest corollary and still
provides an explicit represented completion point through
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.OnePointCompletion.addsPoint}.  The theorem does
not by itself decide infinite-base examples.  The following independent
criterion does.

\begin{theorem}[Old-fixing-context properness]
\label{thm:old-fixing-proper-completion}
Let $D$ be any binary partial algebra; no finiteness assumption is made on its
old carrier.  Suppose
\[
  \eval_D(g,a,b)=\operatorname{none}
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  \eval_D(u,x,e)=\operatorname{some}(x)\qquad(x\in A)
\]
for some $g,u\in\Sigma$ and $a,b,e\in A$.  Define
\[
  c_0=\susp(g,\old(a),\old(b)),\qquad
  c_{k+1}=\susp(u,c_k,\old(e)).
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
  \item $c_1\ne c_2$ but $c_1\stageeq{0}c_2$;
  \item for every $m$, the distinct pair
        $c_{(m+2)!},c_{(m+3)!}$ agrees at stage $m$, and its least finite-tag
        separation rank is greater than $m$;
  \item $(c_{k!})_{k\in\mathbb N}$ is Cauchy and has no generated limit.
\end{enumerate}
Consequently the generated filtered space is not complete, the finite-tag
separation ranks are unbounded, and the canonical completion embedding is not
surjective.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $T$ be a stage-$m$ preserving observer and let
$F(s)=u_T(s,\old(e))$.  Preservation gives
$F(\old(x))=\old(x)$ for every $x\in A$.  For this orbit, collapse the entire
old carrier to one settled state while retaining the $m$ fresh tags and
overflow.  The collapse commutes with $F$ and has $m+2$ states.  Equality in
the collapsed orbit lifts to the original orbit: complement states were
retained injectively, while an orbit that reaches an old value $\old(x)$ stays
at that exact value because $F(\old(x))=\old(x)$.  Finite-orbit periodicity
therefore implies that sufficiently late indices whose difference is divisible
by $(m+2)!$ have equal $T$-values.  Applying this simultaneously to factorial
indices proves that $(c_{k!})$ is Cauchy and gives the stage-$m$ agreement in
clause~(b).  Constructor count proves the pair distinct.  Since a distinct
pair agrees at stage $m$, the exact rank characterization makes its least
separating budget greater than $m$.

At stage zero the complement consists only of overflow.  If $F$ sends overflow
to an old value, the next application fixes that value; if it sends overflow
to overflow, the same table entry fixes it.  Thus $F^2=F$, proving
$c_1\stageeq{0}c_2$, while constructor count again proves $c_1\ne c_2$.

For non-convergence, fix a proposed finite Answer $x$.  The standard
candidate-tailored preserving observer encodes $x$ together with a repeated
$u(-,e)$ prefix longer than $x$.  The next iterate is absent from the selected
finite table and therefore enters overflow; overflow is absorbing along the
remaining tail.  The candidate remains encoded by a non-overflow state, so one
finite stage separates $x$ from every sufficiently late factorial term.  Hence
no generated Answer is a limit.  The checked public statements are
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.OldFixingContextCompletion.factorialSequenceCauchy},
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.OldFixingContextCompletion.factorialSequenceNoLimit},
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.OldFixingContextCompletion.separationRankGreaterThanBudget},
and
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.OldFixingContextCompletion.embeddingNotSurjective}.
\end{proof}

Natural and integer arithmetic both satisfy the hypotheses with undefined
seed $0/0$ and old-fixing context $x\mapsto x+0$.  Thus
\cref{thm:old-fixing-proper-completion} proves that both arithmetic completion
embeddings are non-surjective and that neither filtration has a finite stage
equal to equality.  The direct public corollaries include
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.NatDivision.oldFixingCriterion},
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.NatDivision.separationRanksUnbounded},
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.IntDivision.completionEmbeddingNotSurjective}, and
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.IntDivision.separationRanksUnbounded}.

\begin{proposition}[Incomparability of the properness hypotheses]
\label{prop:properness-hypotheses-incomparable}
Neither properness hypothesis subsumes the other.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item The one-point always-undefined algebra has a one-state code, an
        undefined old application, and a proper completion, but it admits no
        old-fixing-context witness.
  \item Natural arithmetic has the old-fixing witness $x\mapsto x+0$ and a
        proper completion, but its old carrier admits no finite code.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For the one-point algebra, any old-fixing witness would require its sole
operation to satisfy
$\eval_D(u,\star,\star)=\operatorname{some}(\star)$, contradicting the definition
$\eval_D\equiv\operatorname{none}$.  Its one-state code, undefined operation,
and proper completion are explicit.

For natural arithmetic, the witness is the undefined seed $0/0$ together with
the context $x\mapsto x+0$.  If a finite code of bound $m$ existed, applying
the bounded pigeonhole theorem to the $m+1$ inputs $0,\ldots,m$ would give
distinct $i,j$ with equal codes, contradicting injectivity.  The checked
statements are
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.PropernessCriteria.onePointProperWithoutOldFixing}
and
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.PropernessCriteria.natProperWithoutFiniteCoding}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Completed operations]
Every generated operation $f_{\RAlg{D}}$ is jointly stage-compatible and
therefore extends pointwise to a unique jointly nonexpansive operation
\[
  \widehat f:
  \Comp{\RAlg{D}}\times\Comp{\RAlg{D}}
  \longrightarrow\Comp{\RAlg{D}}.
\]
For embedded points,
\[
  \widehat f(\embed x,\embed y)
  =\embed(f_{\RAlg{D}}(x,y)).
\]
\end{proposition}

The completed carrier and operations form a compatible total extension of $D$
with injective old embedding.
Its completeness is exposed by
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.completionComplete}; the compatible algebra is
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.completedAlgebra}.

\subsection{Relative universal property}

The formalization defines a category-like class of targets consisting of:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a compatible total extension of $D$ with injective old embedding;
  \item a separated descending filtration;
  \item nonexpansive generated interpretation;
  \item jointly nonexpansive primitive operations;
  \item completeness of the filtered carrier.
\end{itemize}
For every such target $T$, generated interpretation extends uniquely from the
stage-dense subalgebra, and binary-extension uniqueness shows that the extension
preserves all primitive operations.  Hence the completion is initial relative
to this specified target class.  The exact Lean package is
\leanname{Resolution.External.FilteredTotalAlg.completedResolutionFilteredAlgebra\_initial}.

The adjectives “initial,” “reflective,” and “idempotent” are mathematically
correct only with these hypotheses.  They are consequences of standard
completion arguments and are not presented here as separate novelty claims.

\section{Exact Equational Conservativity}\label{sec:equations}

Let algebraic terms contain variables, named old-carrier constants, and binary
operation symbols.  They can be evaluated in $\RAlg{D}$ and in
$\Comp{\RAlg{D}}$.  Embedding commutes with term evaluation by induction.
Moreover, every term operation preserves each stage relation because the
primitive operations do.

Fix a completed assignment $\rho$ and a stage $n$.  Stage-density allows us to
choose, independently for every variable, a generated assignment $\rho_n$ with
\[
  \rho(v)\stageeq{n}\embed(\rho_n(v)).
\]
Stage compatibility then gives
\[
  \llbracket s\rrbracket_{\rho}
  \stageeq{n}
  \embed\bigl(\llbracket s\rrbracket_{\rho_n}\bigr)
\]
and similarly for $t$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact equation preservation]
\label{thm:equational-conservativity}
For every constant-bearing equation $s=t$,
\[
  \left(\forall\rho:\operatorname{Var}\to\RAlg{D},\
    \llbracket s\rrbracket_\rho=\llbracket t\rrbracket_\rho\right)
\]
if and only if
\[
  \left(\forall\widehat\rho:\operatorname{Var}\to\Comp{\RAlg{D}},\
    \llbracket s\rrbracket_{\widehat\rho}
    =\llbracket t\rrbracket_{\widehat\rho}\right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For the forward implication, approximate an arbitrary completed assignment at
every stage.  The generated equation identifies the two embedded approximants,
so the completed term values agree at every stage.  Separatedness yields exact
equality.  The reverse implication follows by evaluating on embedded generated
assignments and using injectivity of the embedding.
\end{proof}

The public theorem is
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.equationConservative}.

\begin{remark}[Conditional equations]
The theorem concerns universal equations.  Arbitrary quasi-equations do not
follow from density alone.  The formal development transfers conditional laws
only under explicit stage-robust or dense-premise hypotheses.  No unrestricted
quasi-equational conservativity claim is made.
\end{remark}

\section{Arithmetic Examples}\label{sec:arithmetic}

The general construction does not depend on ring or field axioms.  We include
natural-number and integer instances because they make the behavior at a zero
denominator concrete.

\subsection{Natural numbers}

Take operations $+$, $\cdot$, and $/$ on $\mathbb N$, with division undefined
exactly when the denominator is zero.  For $b\ne0$,
\[
  \operatorname{divide}(a,b)=\old(a/b).
\]
At zero,
\[
  \operatorname{divide}(a,0)
  =\susp(\mathsf{div},\old(a),\old(0)).
\]
Therefore
\[
  a/0=b/0\Longrightarrow a=b,
\]
and no $a/0$ is an old natural number.  These properties remain true after the
completion embedding.  They are exposed by
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.NatDivision.singularFamilyInjective} and
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.NatDivision.singularFamilyDisjoint}.

The observational completion is nontrivial in a stronger sense.  By
\cref{thm:old-fixing-proper-completion}, repeatedly adding the old right identity to
$0/0$ produces distinct finite Answers that no fixed observation stage can
fully separate.  The factorially sampled family is Cauchy without a generated
limit, so the completion contains an explicit point outside the generated
image, and the explicit pair at budget $m$ has separation rank greater than
$m$.  This uses the old-fixing-context criterion rather than the finite-base
theorem.

\subsection{Integers}

The integer instance additionally includes subtraction.  Again, every nonzero
division agrees with the old evaluator, while
\[
  a/0=\susp(\mathsf{div},\old(a),\old(0)).
\]
The completed singular family is injective, and completed $0/0$ is not any
embedded integer.  The paper-facing results are
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.IntDivision.singularFamilyInjective} and
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.IntDivision.zeroDivZeroNotOld}.

Integer addition also has old right identity zero.  Hence the same
old-fixing-context criterion proves that every finite integer-arithmetic stage
is strictly coarser than equality, its separation ranks are unbounded, and its
completion embedding is not surjective.  These corollaries are
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.IntDivision.everyFiniteStageNotEquality},
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.IntDivision.factorialOldFixingCauchy}, and
\leanname{ResolutionSemantics.IntDivision.completionEmbeddingNotSurjective}.

\begin{resultbox}
The construction does not assign an ordinary numerical value to $0/0$.
It gives $0/0$ a structured Answer in a conservative extension.
\end{resultbox}

This distinction matters when comparing with totalized arithmetic.  In an
involutive meadow one usually sets $0^{-1}=0$; in a common meadow exceptional
terms propagate one designated error; wheels adopt a different algebraic
extension with infinity- and error-like behavior
\cite{BergstraHirshfeldTucker2009,BergstraPonseCommonMeadows,Carlstrom2004}.
Resolution chooses provenance retention rather than a fixed old value or one
undifferentiated error.

\section{Relation to Prior Work}\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Free completion and partial algebras}

The closest structural prior art is the free completion of partial algebras and
the Schmidt construction \cite{ClimentVidalCosmeLlopez2023}.  Theorem
\ref{thm:free-completion} should be read as a Lean-checked concrete
presentation of that general idea for the one-sorted binary setting.  Other
completion and encoding results show that partial-to-total extension is not in
itself a novel contribution
\cite{Diaconescu2009,BethkeKlopDeVrijer1996,BethkeKlopDeVrijer1999,Nesin2011}.
In particular, \cref{sec:diaconescu-bridge} follows Diaconescu's choices of
$\Gamma$, $\alpha$, $\beta$, and $\gamma$ and checks them in the repository's
concrete typed syntax.  Its value here is the verified interface between that
established encoding and Resolution normal forms, not a priority claim.

\subsection{Ground terms, recognizability, and operation trees}

Separating operation syntax from interpretation is standard in term algebras,
free monads, and algebraic-effects semantics \cite{PlotkinPower2003}.  We do
not claim the first operation-tree semantics.  The specific normalization
discipline is: reduce an old-defined node immediately and retain an
old-undefined node with its already resolved children.

For finite bases and finite signatures this normalization is also a finite
ground-equational presentation, as detailed in
\cref{rem:ground-equation-comparison}.  Recognizable tree languages, syntactic
congruences, and finite tree automata are therefore direct prior art for the
ordinary residual-finiteness consequence
\cite{Kozen1977,Brainerd1969,DauchetTison1990,ComonEtAl2008,
ClimentVidalCosmeLlopez2020}.  The pair-dependent separator likewise uses the
standard finite closure of selected subterms plus a sink-like state.  The
distinguishing constraint in this paper is not that combinatorial pattern but
its compatibility with a possibly infinite base that is fixed pointwise while
only the complement is budgeted.

\subsection{Tree metrics and completion}

Ultrametric and algebraic treatments of infinite trees long predate this work
\cite{ArnoldNivat1980,Courcelle1983}.  Our completion is distinguished by its
source of finite observations: agreement in all compatible finite-tag models,
rather than prefix depth.  The Cauchy-sequence quotient and continuous
extension arguments are standard once the separated filtration is available.

The distinction is not a matter of presentation.  Write $x\equiv^{d}_{n}y$ for
agreement of two Answers on all levels strictly above depth $n$; this is the
filtration whose completion is the classical infinite-tree space.  The two
uniformities differ, and a single family of witnesses shows it.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:depth-observation-contrast}
Let $D$ be a binary partial algebra with
$\eval_D(f,a,z)=\operatorname{none}$ for some $f$, $a$, $z$.  Define the
\emph{combs} by $c_0=a$ and $c_{k+1}=f(c_k,z)$.  Then the sequence
$(\Res_D c_k)_{k\in\mathbb N}$ is Cauchy for the filtration
$\equiv^{d}_{n}$ but not Cauchy for the filtration $\stageeq{n}$.
\end{proposition}

The formal statement is
\leanname{Resolution.Probe.depthCauchy\_not\_observationalCauchy}.  Each
comb suspends at every level, so $\Res_D c_i$ and $\Res_D c_j$ agree above depth
$n$ whenever $i,j\ge n$: under the tree metric the sequence converges to the
infinite comb.  For the finite-tag filtration one preserving model with a
\emph{single} fresh tag suffices to break it.  Send an undefined old pair to the
tag, the tag to $\overflow$, and $\overflow$ back to the tag; the fold along the
combs then alternates between the tag and $\overflow$, so adjacent combs are
separated at stage $1$ for every $k$, however deep the disagreement lies
(\leanname{Resolution.Probe.comb\_separatesAt\_one}).  A fixed observational
budget therefore sees through unbounded depth, which prefix truncation cannot
do.  Consequently the identity on $\RAlg{D}$ does not carry
$\equiv^{d}_{n}$-Cauchy sequences to $\stageeq{n}$-Cauchy ones, and no uniformly
continuous isomorphism of the two completions commutes with the canonical
embeddings.  Only the statement about the two filtrations is formalized; the
consequence for the completions is the standard argument on uniformly
continuous maps and is not machine-checked.

In the arithmetic instance of \cref{sec:arithmetic} the effect is sharper: the
combs are the iterated singular divisions $(\cdots((a/0)/0)\cdots/0)$, and
\emph{no fresh tags at all} are needed.  Sending each singular division to the
successor of its numerator is a preserving model over
$\mathbb N\sqcup\{\overflow\}$ that separates every pair of distinct combs at
stage $0$, letting the old carrier itself do the counting
(\leanname{Resolution.Probe.NatProbe.natComb\_pairwise\_separated\_at\_zero}).

That stage-zero separator does not make the full natural-arithmetic filtration
discrete.  It exploits fresh old naturals generated by the iterated-division
table.  The different family $c_0=0/0$, $c_{k+1}=c_k+0$ cannot use such tags:
preservation forces $a+0=a$ on every old natural.  In fact $c_1$ and $c_2$ agree
in every stage-zero observer, and \cref{thm:old-fixing-proper-completion} gives an
indistinguishable distinct pair at every finite stage.

The iterated-division comb also bears on the sharpness of the bound in
\cref{thm:finite-external-separation}.  Since $|\Res_D c_k|=2k+1$, that bound
permits $4k+4$ tags to separate $c_k$ from $c_{k+1}$, whereas one tag suffices
in general and none in arithmetic.  The linear budget is therefore far from
tight on deep pairs; we do not know its exact order of growth.

\subsection{Finite complements and profinite comparison}

Finite-complement extensions are classical in semigroup theory under the name
of finite Rees index.  Cain and Maltcev's survey explicitly records that
residual finiteness passes both to finite-Rees-index subsemigroups and to their
finite-complement extensions, citing the theorem of Ru\v{s}kuc and Thomas
\cite{CainMaltcev2013,RuskucThomas1998}.  Thus residual finiteness is not a gap
in that literature.  Miller, O'Reilly, Quick, and Ru\v{s}kuc study residual,
weak, strong, and complete separability conditions for semigroups in the
finite-quotient tradition \cite{MillerOReillyQuickRuskuc2022}.

These results are important adjacent prior art, but neither citation by itself
identifies the observer class used here.  Our base is a pointwise-fixed partial
subalgebra, while a separator is a compatible total extension whose complement
over that base is finite; the separating codomain need not itself be finite
when the base is infinite.  Moreover, ordinary Rees index starts with a closed
subsemigroup.  By \cref{prop:old-image-closed}, the old image in the generated
Answer algebra is closed under the total operations exactly in the already
total case.  Hence finite Rees index is not literally the inclusion used by a
genuinely partial Resolution algebra.  The remaining specialist question is
whether separation by finite-complement extensions of a pointwise-fixed
\emph{partial} base has already been studied under a generalized Rees-index,
relative residual-finiteness, or separability formulation.

There is likewise a mature theory of profinite completions and natural
extensions of residually finite algebras, as well as categorical formulations
through profinite monads \cite{DaveyGouveiaHaviarPriestley2011,
ChenAdamekMiliusUrbat2016}.  Recent work on profinite trees gives a particularly
close comparison on the tree side \cite{Moreau2024}.  Those constructions are
organized by finite quotients or finite recognizing algebras.  Our stages
instead allow a possibly infinite preserved base and bound only the genuinely
new complement.  The finite-base properness theorem above is therefore not
presented as a theorem about ordinary profinite completion.  The documented
search has found these analogues, but no exact equivalence or prior statement
of the criterion; specialist review is still required.

\subsection{Totalized division}

Meadows, common meadows, wheels, non-involutive meadows, partial meadows, and
fracpair constructions provide mature alternatives with strong equational and
initial-algebra results
\cite{BergstraTucker2007,BergstraHirshfeldTucker2009,
BergstraMiddelburg2015,BergstraPonseCommonMeadows,Carlstrom2004,
BergstraPonse2016}.  Resolution is not proposed as uniformly superior.  Its
design goal is different: preserve old-defined calculations while retaining
the provenance of old-undefined applications.

\subsection{Evidence-qualified contribution}

The established ingredients include free completion, operation-tree syntax,
finite-signature ground-term recognizability, and standard Cauchy-completion
machinery.  Against that background, the package submitted for specialist
assessment has three parts:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item an intrinsic relative observer class that fixes an arbitrary, possibly
        infinite partial base pointwise and budgets only its complement,
        together with a canonical finite-tag presentation, pairwise separation,
        and an explicit base-faithful construction bound;
  \item the induced observational completion, including an exact finite-base
        properness criterion, an independent arbitrary-base old-fixing-context
        criterion, their formal incomparability, the contrast with prefix-depth
        completion, and exact universal equational conservativity;
  \item an audited Lean development comprising the many-sorted encoding
        bridge and the binary separation--completion results, with natural-
        and integer-arithmetic instances and explicit unbounded-rank
        witnesses.
\end{enumerate}
Our search has not found this exact relative separation--completion package.
That reports the present search result, not proof of historical priority or a
claim that each constituent is new.

\section{Limitations and Open Questions}\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The $\Gamma$--$\alpha$--$\beta$--$\gamma$ bridge is many-sorted and
        supports arbitrary finite arities and separate $TF/PF$ families.  The
        singleton-sort, no-$TF$, binary-$PF$ instance is formally identified
        with the original binary Resolution core.  The finite-complement
        separation and observational-completion layer nevertheless remains
        one-sorted and binary.  Extending that second layer, and comparing it
        directly with the full Schmidt construction, remains open.
  \item The bridge checks the concrete operations-only sentence and model
        constructions used here, including the fixed-signature adjunction laws
        and both initiality transfers.  For the explicit quasi-existence syntax,
        it also constructs the initial translated conditional model and the
        resulting initial partial model.  It does not formalize institution
        morphisms, arbitrary signature morphisms, the institution-level form
        of Corollary~4.2, or Birkhoff proof-theoretic completeness.
  \item For finite bases and finite signatures, ordinary residual finiteness is
        already explained by classical ground-term recognizability.  The
        precise relation of the arbitrary-base, pointwise-preserving
        finite-complement observer class to known relative residual-finiteness
        constructions remains under review.
  \item The linear tag bound is a baseline upper bound, not an optimality
        theorem; its relative state complexity is open.
  \item Properness is classified exactly for finitely coded old carriers.  The
        old-fixing-context hypothesis is sufficient but not necessary, and
        neither of the two stated hypotheses subsumes the other; a full
        arbitrary-carrier classification remains open.
  \item The construction is semantic, not an efficiency claim.  Retaining full
        provenance can produce large finite trees.
  \item No ordinary field law is asserted globally on singular Answers unless
        it is separately proved in the generated algebra.
  \item Constant-bearing universal equations transfer exactly; conditional
        equations require additional hypotheses.
  \item Historical priority for the combined package remains unresolved until
        specialist review is complete.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

The many-sorted bridge places the project explicitly downstream of
Diaconescu's total-algebra encoding: the concrete $TF/PF$ Horn theory, the
$\alpha$--$\beta$ satisfaction condition, and the canonical $\gamma$ lift are
all checked in Lean.  The singleton-sort, no-$TF$, binary-$PF$ instance is
canonically equivalent to the original binary implementation, including its
generated and truth carriers and all encoded operations.  The guarded
conditional translation and term-model
construction also give the fixed-signature initial partial model for the
formalized quasi-existence theories.  Their mathematical priority remains with the
established source.  Resolution semantics is then best understood as a normalized structured
presentation of a free compatible completion of a partial algebra.  Its basic
conservativity follows from the reduction rule.  The substantive additional
construction is intrinsic finite-complement separation over the
pointwise-preserved old fragment.  It separates every generated pair while
adding only finitely many states beyond the base; a derived finite-tag normal
form supplies the explicit witnesses and induces a separated observational
filtration.  For finitely coded bases this implies ordinary residual finiteness
with
an explicit whole-target bound; the qualitative finite-signature consequence
also follows from classical ground-term recognizability.  For genuinely partial
algebras the old image
is not a total subalgebra, so the finite-complement condition is not literally
ordinary Rees index.  The
general linear estimate is a baseline; its sharper relative complexity is
open.  Over every finitely coded old carrier, the completion is proper exactly
when an old application is undefined.  This finite-base hypothesis and the
old-fixing-context hypothesis are incomparable, as witnessed by the one-point
algebra and natural arithmetic respectively.  Standard completion methods then
produce a complete total algebra with canonical operations and exactly the same
universal equations in the language with named old-carrier constants.

The old-fixing-context theorem explains the arithmetic examples uniformly:
for both natural and integer arithmetic every finite stage is strictly coarser
than equality, the separation ranks are unbounded, and the factorial
repeated-$+0$ family supplies an explicit completion point outside the
generated image.  At the same time, $0/0$ remains a structured element outside
the old numerical image; the construction does not replace ordinary
arithmetic with a new numerical convention.  The formal results are
reproducible in Lean, while the novelty claim is intentionally limited to the
specific verified combination described above.
