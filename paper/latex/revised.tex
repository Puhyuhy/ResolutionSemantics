\title{\textbf{Finite-Complement Separation in Partial Algebras}}
\author{Adrian Puha}
\date{21 August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Let $D$ be a partial algebra with carrier $A$.  We consider compatible total
extensions in which the copy of $A$ is preserved pointwise and only finitely
many elements are added outside it.  The carrier $A$ itself may be infinite.

The main result is a finite-family separation theorem.  Given finitely many
distinct elements of the free compatible completion of $D$, there is one such
total extension whose interpretation is injective on that finite set.  The
construction uses only finitely many elements outside $A$.  For an infinite
base this does not follow from the usual product-of-separators argument,
because the class of pointwise-base-preserving finite-complement extensions is
not closed under binary products.

We use these extensions to define a descending family of finite-complement
indistinguishability relations and study the associated completion.  For
finitely coded bases, the completion is proper exactly when the original
algebra is genuinely partial.  We also give a sufficient criterion for
properness over arbitrary bases and illustrate it on natural and integer
arithmetic with division undefined at zero.

All proofs in the paper are given in ordinary mathematical form.  A separate
Lean development is provided only as supplementary machine verification.
\end{abstract}
