\title{\textbf{Resolution Semantics for Partial Algebras}\\
Finite-Complement Observers and Proper Observational Completion}
\author{Adrian Puha}
\date{18 August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Let $D$ be a partial algebra with carrier $A$, where $A$ may be infinite.  We
study total extensions of $D$ that keep every element of $A$ fixed pointwise
and add only finitely many states outside it.  The budget counts new semantic
states, not the size of the model, so the extensions are typically infinite.

Two theorems drive the paper.  \emph{Finite-pattern realization}: every finite
family of terms is separated by a single such observer.  \emph{Finite-pattern
escape}: along a syntactically growing orbit, each proposed limit is refuted by
an observer tailored to it.  Combined with a compression argument, these force
the induced completion to be \emph{proper} --- to contain Cauchy points that
are not terms.  The observer class is not closed under binary products over an
infinite base, so the finite-family theorem cannot be obtained by the usual
product-of-separators argument and is proved by direct construction.

The mechanism is concrete on $\mathbb N$ with division undefined at zero: the
term $0/0$ never becomes a number, and iterating $x\mapsto x+0$ --- a context
fixing every natural --- yields a sequence no finite budget can follow to a
limit.  Separation ranks along it are unbounded.  We also determine exactly
which universal equations survive completion, and locate the construction as
the singleton-sort binary case of the standard many-sorted partial-to-total
encoding.
\end{abstract}
