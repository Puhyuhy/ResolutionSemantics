# Structural core of the current paper

This note records the post-audit reduction of the separation/completion theory
to a small checked structural core.  It is a research map, not an additional
novelty claim.

## I. Relative finite-pattern realization

For any finite list of generated Answers,
`lean/ResolutionFinitePatternRealization.lean` forms the union of their finite
subterm closures and constructs one compatible finite-tag observer that fixes
the entire partial base pointwise, gives selected suspended nodes finite tags,
sends unrelated transitions to overflow, and is injective simultaneously on
the selected generated Answers.

Pairwise separation is the two-point consequence.  The independently checked
`ResolutionFinitaryPatternRealization.lean` generalizes the construction to
arbitrary finite operation arities, including arity zero.  That module is a
generality probe; the promoted manuscript keeps the binary kernel for clarity.

The class is not product-closed over an infinite fixed base.
`ResolutionRelativeProductObstruction.lean` gives an explicit Nat
counterexample, so simultaneous finite-pattern realization cannot be recovered
inside the same observer class merely by multiplying pairwise separators.

## II. Trajectory compression

`lean/ResolutionOrbitCompressionMaster.lean` isolates the dynamic input needed
for the Cauchy argument.  At each observation stage it is enough that the
single deterministic trajectory generated by the chosen unary family admit a
finite code reflecting equality at ordered times.  The whole observer carrier
may remain infinite.

Pigeonhole repetition plus factorial divisibility then makes the factorially
sampled trajectory Cauchy.  This finite-dynamics step is standard machinery and
is not a novelty claim.

Both finite-base and old-fixing infinite-base arguments instantiate this same
abstraction: in the former the whole observer is finite; in the latter only the
relevant trajectory compresses after the pointwise-fixed base is treated as a
settled case.

## III. Relative finite-pattern escape

`lean/ResolutionFinitePatternAntiLimit.lean` handles the logically independent
no-limit half.  For a growing unary syntax

`t_(k+1) = susp(stepOp, t_k, old(fixedRight))`,

and any generated candidate `x`, a finite-pattern observer retains `x` and a
long enough orbit prefix exactly.  The first unseen continuation falls into
overflow, and the chosen unary context keeps overflow absorbing thereafter.
Thus one candidate-dependent observer separates `x` from the entire sufficiently
late tail.

`ResolutionRelativeEscapeComparison.lean` clarifies the relation with ordinary
profinite recognition.  If the retained base is not finitely codable, no
finite target can contain it injectively; the actual escape observer is
therefore globally infinite even though only finitely many states lie outside
the base.  The novelty candidate is this relative realization over a fully
retained base, not candidate-tail separation in the abstract.

## IV. Compression--escape properness

The two independent mechanisms combine as

`finite trajectory compression`

`+ relative finite-pattern escape`

`=> factorial Cauchy + no generated limit`

`=> incomplete generated filtered space`

`=> non-surjective completion embedding`.

This is the structural center of the current paper.  Finite-base properness and
the arbitrary-base old-fixing theorem are checked instances of this mechanism.

## V. Sharper old-fixing synchronization

At stage `n`, an old-fixing orbit either enters the retained base and becomes
constant or remains in the `n` finite tags plus overflow.  The sharper checked
witness is therefore

`c_(n+1)! ~_n c_(n+2)!`,

and the least finite-tag separation ranks are unbounded.

## VI. Distinction from prefix-depth completion

The explicit comb probe in `ResolutionCompletionProbe.lean` is Cauchy under
prefix-depth agreement but not under Resolution observation.  A single one-tag
observer distinguishes every adjacent comb by alternating tag and overflow at
arbitrary depth.  In the Nat instance, a zero-tag observer separates all
distinct combs using only the pointwise-retained numerical base.  Hence the two
uniformities are genuinely different.

## VII. Formal API naming

The Lean namespace `ResolutionSemantics.MasterTheorems` and the filename
`ResolutionOrbitCompressionMaster.lean` are retained as stable development API
identifiers.  The manuscript and review documentation use descriptive theorem
names instead of "Master I/II" terminology.

## Novelty calibration

The established/background components include free compatible completion,
abstract finite-set discrimination, selected-subterm sink constructions in
isolation, finite-state eventual periodicity, factorial synchronization,
generic Cauchy completion, and Diaconescu's encoding.

The strongest contribution under review is the combined relative setting:
pointwise preservation of a possibly infinite partial base, finite complexity
only outside that base, direct simultaneous finite-pattern realization in a
non-product-closed observer class, finite compression of relevant trajectories,
and relative escape witnesses that force proper observational completions.
