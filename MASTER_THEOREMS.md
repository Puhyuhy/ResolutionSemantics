# Master-theorem reduction research branch

Base commit: `67218af8cba8d61100288cd7f8422b7f3fa643da`

This branch records the post-audit reduction of the separation/completion story to a small structural core.

## I. Relative finite-pattern realization

The pairwise separator of Theorem 3.3 has been generalized and Lean-checked.

For any finite list of generated Answers, `lean/ResolutionFinitePatternRealization.lean` forms the union of their finite subterm closures and constructs one compatible finite-tag observer that:

- fixes the entire partial base pointwise;
- gives selected suspended nodes first-occurrence finite tags;
- sends unrelated transitions to one overflow state;
- evaluates every selected normal node to its encoding;
- is injective simultaneously on all generated Answers in the chosen finite list.

Thus pairwise finite-tag separation is the two-point instance of a stronger finite-pattern theorem. The public API is `lean/ResolutionFinitePatternRealizationPublic.lean`, and `ResolutionMasterTheorems.relativeFinitePatternRealization` exposes it as Master I for the main binary kernel.

### Verified arbitrary-finitary generalization

`lean/ResolutionFinitaryPatternRealization.lean` independently checks that the same mechanism does not depend on binarity. In that module a signature consists of operation symbols with arbitrary finite arities `arity : Op -> Nat`, raw suspended nodes carry a `Fin (arity f)`-indexed family of children, and partial evaluation accepts the corresponding finite tuple.

For any finite child-closed list of normalized raw Answers, one compatible finite-complement observer is constructed that preserves every defined old/base application and is injective on the whole selected pattern. The proof works for every finite arity, including arity zero.

This finitary module is deliberately a generality probe rather than a replacement of the manuscript's binary kernel. Its purpose is to establish that the finite-tag + overflow realization principle is structural, not a consequence of binary syntax.

## II. Finite trajectory compression

Master II's dynamic half is abstracted and Lean-checked in `lean/ResolutionOrbitCompressionMaster.lean`.

The correct abstraction is weaker than finiteness of an observer carrier. At each finite observation stage, it is enough that the **single deterministic trajectory generated by the chosen unary family** admits a uniformly bounded finite code such that equal codes at ordered times reflect equality of the actual observer states.

From that hypothesis alone, the standard pigeonhole/periodicity argument gives a repeated orbit state and factorial divisibility synchronizes all sufficiently late factorial samples. Therefore the factorially sampled generated family is Cauchy at every finite observation stage.

This dynamic half is intentionally not presented as the novel part: finite-state eventual periodicity and factorial synchronization are standard finite/profinite orbit technology.

### Verified instances of trajectory compression

Both previous Cauchy mechanisms are Lean-checked instances of the same abstraction:

1. **finite base:** the whole stage observer is finite, so the relevant orbit is automatically finitely coded;
2. **old-fixing infinite base:** the full carrier may be infinite, but collapsing the pointwise-fixed old base leaves a finite trajectory quotient consisting of one settled-old state, the finite tags, and overflow.

Thus the common mechanism is not "finite carrier" but **finite complexity of the observer trajectory relevant to the chosen syntactic orbit**.

## III. Finite-pattern escape: the anti-limit principle

The Resolution-specific no-limit mechanism has now also been abstracted and Lean-checked in `lean/ResolutionFinitePatternAntiLimit.lean`.

Suppose a generated unary orbit has growing syntax of the form

`t_(k+1) = susp(stepOp, t_k, old(fixedRight))`.

For any candidate generated Answer `x`, choose a finite pattern containing `x` and an orbit prefix longer than `x`. The finite-pattern observer realizes this entire pattern exactly. The next orbit node has larger constructor count than every subtree of either retained root, so it cannot already belong to the selected pattern. Consequently the next context transition misses the finite table and is sent to overflow.

The same finite-pattern construction proves that no selected transition can have overflow as an encoded left child. Hence overflow is absorbing under all further applications of the chosen unary context. Every sufficiently late orbit term therefore evaluates to overflow, while the candidate evaluates to its non-overflow selected encoding.

This yields a generic theorem:

`growing unary syntax + finite-pattern realization`

`=> factorial sample has no generated limit`.

Importantly, **trajectory compression is not used in this no-limit proof**. Conversely, finite-pattern escape is not used in the Cauchy proof. The two mechanisms are logically independent.

## IV. Combined properness theorem

The two independent halves now combine into one Lean-checked structural theorem:

`uniform finite trajectory compression`

`+ finite-pattern escape of the growing unary syntax`

`=> factorial sample is Cauchy and has no generated limit`

`=> generated filtered space is incomplete`

`=> canonical completion embedding is not surjective`.

This theorem is exposed through `ResolutionMasterTheorems.compressedEscapingOrbitNotComplete` and `ResolutionMasterTheorems.compressedEscapingOrbitEmbeddingNotSurjective`.

Both the finite-base undefined comb and the old-fixing infinite-base orbit instantiate this same combined theorem. The previous candidate-tailored no-limit proofs are therefore instances of one finite-pattern anti-limit principle rather than separate ad hoc constructions.

This is the cleanest current structural explanation of properness in the paper:

- **compression** makes an infinite syntactic orbit observationally Cauchy;
- **escape** guarantees that no finite generated Answer can absorb that orbit as a limit.

The tension between those two properties forces genuinely new points in the observational completion.

## Verified sharper old-fixing bound

The original proof collapsed the pointwise-fixed base to one settled state, producing an `(m+2)`-state quotient and the witness pair

`c_(m+2)!  ~_m  c_(m+3)!`.

The research branch contains a Lean-checked sharper proof in `lean/ResolutionOldFixingContextSharperOrbit.lean`.

A stage-m observer has only `m+1` states outside the base (`m` tags plus overflow). The proof splits an observer orbit into two exhaustive cases:

- if the orbit enters the base, the base-fixing law makes it constant forever;
- otherwise the orbit remains in the `m+1` external states, so a repetition occurs by pigeonhole and its period divides `(m+1)!`.

Consequently the stronger statement is formally verified:

`c_(m+1)!  ~_m  c_(m+2)!`.

The public research API exposes both this stage-equivalence theorem and the resulting strict separation-rank bound

`m < rank(c_(m+1)!, c_(m+2)!)`.

The `m = 0` instance yields `c_1 ~_0 c_2`, so the former separate stage-zero phenomenon is subsumed by the sharper general orbit theorem.

## Current checked architecture

The branch now supports:

- **Master I — Relative finite-pattern realization:** one finite-complement observer simultaneously realizes every chosen finite pattern of generated Answers in the main binary kernel;
- the same finite-pattern realization mechanism is independently Lean-checked for arbitrary finite operation arities;
- Theorem 3.3's pairwise finite-tag statement is the two-point consequence of Master I;
- **Master II — Finite trajectory compression:** uniform finite coding of the relevant observer trajectory forces factorial Cauchy behavior without requiring the whole observer carrier to be finite;
- **Finite-pattern escape:** growing unary syntax admits candidate-tailored finite observers that force every sufficiently long continuation into absorbing overflow, ruling out every generated limit;
- **Combined properness:** compression plus escape forces the observational completion to add points;
- finite-base and old-fixing infinite-base properness are both instances of this combined theorem;
- the old-fixing unbounded-rank witness is sharpened from `(m+2)!/(m+3)!` to `(m+1)!/(m+2)!`;
- factorial periodicity is explicitly treated as standard machinery rather than a novelty claim.

## Novelty calibration

The reduction does not say that the paper has no novelty. It identifies where novelty claims are defensible.

The standard part is finite deterministic dynamics: pigeonhole repetition, eventual periodicity, and factorial synchronization.

The structurally distinctive part is the relative observational setting: the entire partial base is preserved pointwise while only external complexity is budgeted; finite patterns can be realized exactly even over an infinite base; relevant observer trajectories may nevertheless compress to finite quotients; and the same finite-pattern machinery can force syntactic escape beyond every finite candidate. Their interaction produces proper completions even when the old carrier itself is infinite.

That interaction — finite observational compression together with finite-pattern anti-limit escape — is the strongest current candidate for the paper's central structural contribution.
